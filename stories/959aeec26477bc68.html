The odds against Tinubu, By Wole Olaoye

2026-10-25

As Nigeria approaches its next presidential elections, President Bola Ahmed Tinubu is considered a leading candidate, bolstered by control over the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). Despite his incumbency, there are significant challenges ahead, primarily stemming from the potential for a strategic coalition among major opposition figures, including Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi.

If opposition candidates can unite around a single consolidated ticket, they could effectively pool their voting bases, significantly amplifying their chances against Tinubu. This coalition has the potential to cover key demographic areas, such as the South-East and South-South regions, urban centers, and strongholds in the North. In the previous election, the split opposition vote among various parties diluted their impact, highlighting the necessity for a unified front to challenge the current power dynamics.

However, forming such a coalition is fraught with challenges, chiefly the question of who will lead the ticket. Atiku, the seasoned veteran, may alienate Obi's supporters if he heads the campaign, while placing Obi in the lead could provoke resistance from traditional political elites in the North. Both factions have their vested interests, and the reluctance to compromise could thwart efforts to establish a viable opposition coalition. The internal discord among state-level party factions further complicates these negotiations, where local rivalries may impede larger strategic goals.

Additionally, the political landscape is influenced by ethnic and regional dynamics, which can affect alliances and voter loyalty. Both the Yoruba and Igbo populations have seen attempts to incite division among them, underscoring the importance of unity among opposition constituents.

In light of these complexities, the opposition must either come together cohesively to present a compelling alternative or risk remaining fragmented and ineffective in the face of the formidable Tinubu and his ruling party. Time is running short, with less than three months until the elections, necessitating swift and decisive action to reshape Nigeria's political fabric.
