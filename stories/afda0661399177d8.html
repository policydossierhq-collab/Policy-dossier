Tinubu acknowledges challenges facing Nigerian youths, promises more opportunities

2026-11-09

President Bola Tinubu has acknowledged concerns among young Nigerians about unemployment, the cost of living, access to education, business opportunities and insecurity. Speaking at the closing ceremony of the 2026 National Youth Conference in Abuja on Monday, he said the government had a responsibility to address those expectations. His remarks were delivered by House of Representatives Speaker Tajudeen Abbas.

Tinubu said young people wanted a country where effort is rewarded, talent is recognised and opportunities do not depend on personal connections or background. He said his administration’s Renewed Hope Agenda was intended to build an economy that creates opportunities and rewards enterprise. While recognising that economic reforms had involved sacrifices, he said young people should see future opportunities become tangible improvements in their lives.

The economic pressures have been compounded by the removal of petrol subsidies and the floating of the naira, as well as disruptions to global energy and commodity markets linked to conflicts and wars. Insecurity in parts of Nigeria has also disrupted farming and commerce, displaced communities and contributed to school closures, prolonging educational disruption for millions of children.

Tinubu pointed to several government programmes aimed at widening opportunities, including the Nigerian Education Loan Fund, the Three Million Technical Talent programme, YouthCred and the Nigerian Youth Academy. He said the government would work to ensure skills training leads to employment, innovation attracts investment and young entrepreneurs can continue to access financing. He also pledged support for young Nigerians seeking to succeed, regardless of their background or location.
