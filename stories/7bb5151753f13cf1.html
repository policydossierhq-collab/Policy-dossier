Diri unveils AI robotic tutors in Bayelsa medical university

2026-10-26

Bayelsa Governor Douye Diri has unveiled AI-powered robotic tutors and other digital learning initiatives at Bayelsa Medical University (BMU) in Yenagoa. The unveiling took place alongside the university’s 2025–2029 Strategic Plan as part of efforts to advance medical education, research and technology in the state.

Diri said the tools should make learning more interactive and improve teaching, learning and research, while complementing rather than replacing classroom instruction and lecturers’ mentorship. He challenged the university’s management to turn the strategic plan into measurable gains in academic standards, research, infrastructure and graduate quality. He also reaffirmed state support for BMU’s ASPIRE Agenda, which includes academic excellence, sustainability, partnerships and engagement, innovation and technology, and research.

Vice-Chancellor Dimie Ogoina said the university aims to become a globally recognised institution for medical education, research and innovation. He reported that BMU had secured accreditation for nine academic programmes and that its nursing science students achieved a 96 per cent pass rate in professional examinations. The university has also established a virtual reality, artificial intelligence and mixed-reality studio, expanded digital connectivity and introduced an application for confidential communication between students, staff and university leadership. It is pursuing plans for a smart campus and centres focused on digital health, biomedical innovation, and health, environment and development.

Diri urged BMU to work with the state Ministries of Education and Health to deliver its objectives. He said the Sampou campus was nearing completion and would provide room for expansion. During his visit, he also inspected the newly established teaching hospital, which he said had facilities for X-rays and advanced scans, including CT scans. He said these services could improve access to specialised diagnostics and reduce the need for residents to travel outside Bayelsa for some procedures.
