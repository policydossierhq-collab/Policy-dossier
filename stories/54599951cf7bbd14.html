Atiku’s fuel subsidy reversal plan not realistic — Fashola

2026-11-17

Babatunde Fashola has called Atiku Abubakar’s proposal to restore fuel subsidy unrealistic, saying the former vice-president has not explained how it would be funded. Fashola raised questions about the country’s oil production, security problems in oil-producing areas and the availability of crude for local refineries. He spoke in an interview on Channels Television’s Politics Today on Tuesday.

Fashola said Nigeria was not meeting its crude production targets and pointed to security challenges and vandalism as factors affecting output. He questioned where enough crude would come from to supply refineries and support the proposed arrangement, and said Atiku needed to show that the plan was workable. Fashola stressed that his criticism concerned the proposal, not Atiku personally.

He also questioned the former vice-president’s shift in position, noting that Atiku had supported subsidy removal during the 2023 presidential campaign. Fashola said a reversal would require lawmakers to amend the existing legal framework and asked whether the National Assembly would back the change, given its possible effects on public finances.

Drawing on his experience in the Buhari administration, Fashola said subsidy payments had previously left state governments with less funding. He questioned whether a return to the policy could again constrain funds available to states.

Atiku announced in August 2026 that he would restore the subsidy if elected president in 2027. He proposed a temporary, audited arrangement for petroleum products refined locally, saying it could reduce fuel and transport costs and ease household pressures. President Tinubu announced the removal of the subsidy on May 29, 2023, as part of his administration’s economic reforms.
