2027: CJN warns judges against pressure, conflicting orders

2026-10-27

Chief Justice of Nigeria Kudirat Kekere-Ekun has urged heads of courts to guard against forum shopping, concealed related proceedings and efforts to secure conflicting orders ahead of the 2027 general elections. She spoke in Abuja on Monday at the opening of the 2026 Roundtable for Heads of Courts, organised by the National Judicial Institute.

Kekere-Ekun said election disputes bring intense public interest and pressure, but judges must decide politically sensitive cases impartially and according to the Constitution, the Electoral Act and court rules. She called on court leaders to put administrative arrangements in place for the orderly and timely handling of election cases. Judges, she said, should scrutinise applications, require full disclosure of relevant facts and consider related proceedings, while resisting the misuse of urgent applications or ex parte orders to gain an advantage.

She warned that forum shopping weakens consistency, encourages abuse of court processes and can damage public confidence. No litigant, she said, should be allowed to manipulate the system in search of a favourable decision. The chief justice also identified delays as a major challenge, noting that they impose financial and emotional costs on litigants and can reduce the value of judicial remedies. She urged heads of courts to review their dockets, identify long-pending cases and address avoidable delays through better scheduling, case monitoring and fewer unnecessary adjournments. She cautioned that efficiency should not be measured only by the number of cases resolved: fair hearings and competent decisions must be protected.

Kekere-Ekun also called for transparent, merit-based judicial appointments and urged court leaders to resist external pressure. She directed them to ensure judges start sittings on time and that lawyers and litigants are notified promptly when sessions are cancelled. She said courtrooms should remain professional environments where litigants are treated with dignity and lawyers with courtesy. The CJN further called for investment in judicial support staff and structured mentorship for newly appointed judges. Earlier, NJI Administrator and retired National Industrial Court President Babatunde Adejumo said the roundtable gives heads of courts an opportunity to discuss shared challenges and develop solutions, while noting that the judiciary operates under sustained public scrutiny.
