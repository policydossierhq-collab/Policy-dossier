CBD COP17: Namibian Biosafety Expert Martha Kandawa-Schulz dies in Yerevan

2026-10-25

Namibian biosafety expert Martha Kandawa-Schulz died in Yerevan, Armenia, on 23 October while attending the United Nations Biodiversity Conference, known as CBD COP17. The Convention on Biological Diversity Secretariat announced her death in a statement issued on Sunday evening.

Kandawa-Schulz was Namibia’s Cartagena Protocol National Focal Point and a professor. At the conference, she was serving as co-chair of the Contact Group on Risk Assessment and Risk Management of Living Modified Organisms.

The Secretariat described her as a respected colleague whose expertise, leadership and commitment had contributed to the Convention and its protocols, as well as to the global biosafety community. It extended condolences to her family, friends, colleagues, the Namibian government and people, and the African Group and others mourning her death.

The Secretariat said it was working with the conference presidency to support Kandawa-Schulz’s family and the Namibian delegation and to make the necessary arrangements. A book of condolences was to be available to delegates from 13:00 on Monday at Entrance 2, near the Asia-Pacific Room. The circumstances of her death were not stated; reports indicated that she collapsed during a working group meeting on Friday.

CBD COP17 is being held in Yerevan, where governments and other participants are discussing biodiversity conservation and implementation of the Convention and its protocols. The conference includes government representatives, scientists, civil society representatives and researchers from nearly 196 countries.
