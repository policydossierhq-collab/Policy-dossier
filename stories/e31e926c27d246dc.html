Clashes break out as hundreds of thousands protest in France

2026-10-31

Hundreds of thousands of people took part in nationwide demonstrations in France on Saturday, protesting rising living costs, inequality and police violence. The third consecutive weekend of rallies brought together Yellow Vest activists, labour unions and high school and university students. Some marchers wore Halloween costumes, including as witches, clowns and fictional characters. Authorities counted about 320,000 participants, including 70,000 in Paris, while youth organisations put the national total close to 500,000, with 200,000 in the capital.

Most demonstrations were peaceful, but confrontations broke out in several cities later in the day. In Paris, masked demonstrators at the rear of a march threw paving stones and other projectiles at police, and an incendiary device was also thrown. Police used tear gas and baton charges. The Paris police prefecture described several hundred people as rioters and said officers were acting to restore public order. In Lyon, police fired tear gas after projectiles were thrown. In Toulouse, officers used water cannon against an unauthorised gathering of several thousand people, mostly young demonstrators.

Authorities had mobilised 70,000 police officers and gendarmes nationwide. Officials had warned of possible street violence, recalling unrest two weeks earlier that included looting and fires at public buildings. Some shopkeepers boarded up their premises ahead of the demonstrations. The protests are unfolding less than six months before presidential elections, with far-right leader Marine Le Pen leading opinion polls, according to the source material.

The movement began with demands focused on the cost of living, inequality and worsening conditions in education. After a security crackdown and serious injuries to some protesters, demonstrators also began calling for stronger protection of civil liberties and the right to protest. Student organisers said they planned to blockade high schools and universities next week, then join a November 5 demonstration called by workers and trade unions. The government, facing a weakening economy and pressure to reduce debt, has limited room to respond with additional spending.
