Top 10 most affordable states to live in Nigeria in October 2026

2026-11-17

Taraba had the lowest annual headline inflation rate among Nigerian states in October 2026, at 6.1%, followed by Sokoto at 6.7% and Ekiti at 7.6%. All ten states in the ranking were below the national rate of 14.06%, which fell from 14.63% in September, according to the National Bureau of Statistics’ October consumer price report. National food inflation also eased, to 19.13% from 19.59%.

The remaining states in the top ten were Ogun at 8.5%, Oyo at 8.8%, Osun and Katsina at 9.3%, Jigawa and Ondo at 10.3%, and Cross River at 11.2%. The state-level ranking shifted from September: Taraba retained the top spot, while Bauchi and Adamawa, previously among the three lowest, dropped out of the top ten. The South-West accounted for the largest share of October’s list; no North-Central or South-East state featured.

The figures also show that lower headline inflation did not always mean lower food inflation. Ondo’s food inflation was 31.7%, the highest among the ten states, while Oyo’s was 28.8%. Sokoto’s food inflation rose to 21.4% from 6.1% in September, even as its headline rate fell to 6.7% from 8.0%. Taraba’s food inflation rose from -7.5% to 8.9%, remaining below the national food rate. By contrast, Cross River’s food inflation declined to 16.3% from 21.2%.

The report describes differences in agricultural production, food-market access, transport and distribution networks, and state interventions as factors associated with local price trends. The ranking compares the annual rate of price increases; it does not establish which states have the lowest overall cost of living. Lagos, for example, recorded headline inflation of 15.5% and food inflation of 31.4%, placing 24th nationally.

Source: Nairametrics
