NRS seeks simpler tax rules for creative sector

2026-11-25

The Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) has called for simpler, clearer and more business-friendly tax rules to encourage investment in the country’s creative economy. The appeal was made at a high-level dialogue on taxation and investment during the Creative Africa Nexus (CANEX) event in Lagos. The Service said digital business models, intellectual-property-based enterprises and cross-border transactions require rules that creators, entrepreneurs and investors can understand and navigate.

NRS Executive Chairman Dr. Zacch Adedeji, represented at the dialogue by Executive Director for People, Stakeholders and Communication Obinna Ihedioha, described the creative economy as an important source of employment, foreign exchange earnings, investment and economic opportunity. He said it had grown beyond entertainment as digital platforms changed how creative work is produced and commercialised. Businesses, he noted, increasingly build on copyrights, designs, brands and digital products.

Adedeji said tax administration needs to adapt to these developments without discouraging innovation or business expansion. While government requires sustainable revenue for areas including education, security, technology and infrastructure, he said the design of tax rules can influence whether businesses start, attract investment and grow, or remain small.

He said new tax laws present an opportunity to make the system more efficient through clearer regulations, lower compliance burdens for small businesses and better support for taxpayers. The Service also emphasised that formalising a creative business should offer practical benefits, such as access to finance, contracts, investment, wider markets and legal protection.

NRS activities at CANEX included the high-level dialogue and an Investment and Tax Advisory Hub for creators and investors. The Service also presented the Creative Economy Tax and Investment Initiative (CETII), which it said is intended to maintain engagement beyond the event.
