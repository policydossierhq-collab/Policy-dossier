Were Young People Removed From Nigeria’s Voter Register?

2026-10-25

Nigeria’s voter register grew to 103,428,715 in 2026, up 9,959,707 from 2023. But the number of registered voters aged 18–34 fell from 37,060,399 to 29,777,578, a decline of 7,282,821, or 19.65 per cent. The group’s share of the register dropped from 39.6 per cent to 28.79 per cent.

The decline does not, by itself, show that young people were removed from the register. Three years passed between the two sets of figures, meaning people who were 32, 33 or 34 in 2023 would have moved into the 35–49 age category by 2026. That category grew by 9,852,355 voters, or about 29.5 per cent. Ageing could account for part of the change, but the figures provided do not establish how much.

The overall register increased by 10.66 per cent between 2023 and 2026. For comparison, it grew by 22.04 per cent between 2015 and 2019, and by 11.27 per cent between 2019 and 2023. The latest period covered three years, compared with four years for each of the earlier periods. Although the latest absolute increase was slightly larger than the increase from 2019 to 2023, its percentage growth was lower.

The figures leave questions about how many newly eligible young people registered and whether administrative adjustments affected the totals. Samson Itodo, executive director of Yiaga Africa, has proposed that the Independent National Electoral Commission publish an age-cohort analysis comparing the 2015, 2019, 2023 and 2026 registers. Such an account could set out new registrations, transitions between age groups, removals of deceased or unqualified people, and other adjustments. If youth registration is not keeping pace with those ageing out of the category, the figures point to a need for greater investment in youth registration efforts; if administrative changes explain the decline, those changes should be documented.
