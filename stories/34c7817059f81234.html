Toluwanisings’ death and social media prosecutors, By Festus Adedayo

2026-11-01

Gospel musician Olayemi Bayonile Bolaji, known as Toluwanisings, died on 14 October after collapsing on the staircase of his home in Ibadan, Oyo State. The 34-year-old had recently returned from a worship concert titled In His Presence. His death prompted public concern about the emergency care he received and a dispute over his burial arrangements.

Accounts described in the commentary say he was taken to about seven or eight hospitals in Ibadan, including the University College Hospital, but was either turned away or faced administrative obstacles. The commentary presents his experience as an example of wider concerns about emergency treatment in Nigeria, including allegations that hospitals delay or refuse care. It argues that the focus on the musician’s personal life after his death diverted attention from questions about access to urgent medical help.

The debate also widened into a disagreement over where Toluwanisings should be buried. His widow was said to favour a quiet burial in a modern cemetery, while his father and other relatives reportedly wanted him buried at his newly completed home in Ibadan or beside his late mother in Ikirun, Osun State. The dispute unfolded publicly on social media, alongside claims about his relationship with his family and how he used his money. The account also notes that he left a wife and two young daughters.

The commentary compares the public scrutiny after Toluwanisings’ death with the controversy surrounding singer Mohbad, who died in 2023. In both cases, it says, disputes involving relatives and the handling of the deceased’s remains became prominent in public discussion. It calls for attention to the circumstances of Toluwanisings’ death and criticises the online airing of family conflict, while raising broader questions about emergency hospital care and the treatment of a person’s memory after death.
