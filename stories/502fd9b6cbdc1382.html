2027: How PDP crisis threatens Adebutu’s Ogun gov ambition

2026-11-08

Oladipupo Adebutu’s bid to contest the 2027 Ogun State governorship election is in doubt after the Independent National Electoral Commission omitted him and his running mate, Yemi Sowunmi-Kolapo, from its final list of candidates. INEC said the PDP did not submit its membership register before holding valid primaries. The commission did not add the pair after the 14-day period for appeals expired. Adebutu says he is a registered party member and qualified to contest. He received 262,383 votes in the 2023 governorship election.

The dispute is rooted in a wider PDP leadership crisis, with rival groups led by Abdulrahman Mohammed and Kabiru Turaki each claiming national leadership. INEC recognises the Mohammed-led executive, while both groups held membership registration exercises within the required period. A PDP official in Ogun said Adebutu registered with the Turaki faction and was absent from the digital register submitted by the Mohammed-led group. Adebutu has said he helped organise the party’s registration exercise in Ogun, which he said enrolled more than 19,800 members, and gave his registration number as 1536.

The episode echoes a dispute before the 2019 governorship election, when rival PDP factions held separate primaries. Adebutu won one faction’s ticket, but a Federal High Court directed INEC to recognise Buruji Kashamu as the party’s candidate. Adebutu has accused the APC and its candidate, Olamilekan Adeola, of trying to weaken the opposition. He says the controversy has not slowed the PDP’s preparations and that the party has visited 12 local government areas.

PDP figures in Ogun have expressed confidence that Adebutu will still appear on the ballot, through an administrative resolution or court action. Former Ijebu East local government chairman Wale Adedayo questioned why INEC published 38 of the 40 candidates submitted by the party while excluding Adebutu and his running mate. Board of Trustees member Fasiu Bakene said the party would consider legal action if the names were not restored. The APC, meanwhile, has blamed the PDP’s internal divisions and alleged failure to follow INEC’s membership-registration rules, rejecting claims of interference.
