Evacuated Ebola patient, husband arrive in Switzerland

2026-10-26

Nigeria’s Ebola patient and her husband arrived safely in Switzerland on Monday after a medical evacuation from Abuja, according to Nigeria’s Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Muhammad Pate. The woman is receiving treatment at Geneva University Hospitals. Her husband, identified as a close contact, is in isolation at University Hospital Zurich. Swiss authorities confirmed that both are Swiss citizens and said the woman is being treated for Bundibugyo virus disease, a form of Ebola.

The Swiss Federal Office of Public Health said the hospitals have facilities and trained personnel to manage the cases safely, and that the arrangements pose no public health risk in Switzerland. Pate said Swiss Federal Councillor Elisabeth Baume-Schneider thanked Nigerian doctors, health professionals and authorities during a telephone conversation, and expressed appreciation for cooperation between Nigeria, Switzerland and the World Health Organization. Pate also thanked the teams and agencies involved, including the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention, the WHO and Swiss health authorities.

Nigeria’s response is continuing despite the patient’s evacuation. Pate said contact monitoring, investigation of alerts and other containment measures remain under way, with support from the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention and the WHO Regional Office for Africa. The WHO said Nigeria was monitoring 52 contacts; the NCDC had previously identified 53 primary contacts, including the patient’s husband, healthcare workers, domestic staff and people involved in collecting samples. Authorities are monitoring contacts for symptoms. The WHO said Nigeria had no active confirmed Ebola cases following the evacuation, while Switzerland had taken responsibility for the patient’s care and was monitoring her husband.

Nigeria confirmed its first case on 20 October, after a 42-year-old Ugandan-born woman living in Abuja tested positive. She had returned from Uganda on 3 October and developed symptoms a few days later before being admitted for treatment in isolation. Pate had said Nigeria could declare the outbreak over as early as 7 December if no further confirmed or probable cases were detected.
