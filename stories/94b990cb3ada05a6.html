Ebola: Day pandemic killed an Aláàfin, By Festus Adedayo

2026-10-25

In 1831, a devastating pandemic known as Pẹhẹ claimed the life of Aláàfin Májòótú in Ọyọ-Ilé, highlighting the dire consequences of public health neglect. Historical records indicate that this highly contagious respiratory disease emerged unexpectedly, leading to swift fatalities across the Old Oyo Empire. The tragedy of Aláàfin's death signifies that plagues do not spare even the highest social echelons, a sentiment echoed in traditional Yoruba beliefs about the indiscriminate nature of death.

The pandemic, described in detail by the renowned historian Samuel Johnson, evolved from a local health issue to a widespread crisis by 1831, culminating in the downfall of the sacred palace. As Pẹhẹ spread, it wreaked havoc not only on the populace but also on the political stability of the empire. Alongside a severe famine and ongoing civil strife, the outbreak exacerbated the empire's existing struggles, leading to the deaths of thousands, including many who succumbed due to starvation rather than the disease itself.

Richard Lander, the Cornish explorer, documented the empire's attempts to manage the outbreak, noting the establishment of quarantine measures outside the city. However, the combination of Pẹhẹ with famine and political instability left the local authorities overwhelmed. By the time the pandemic waned, over 100,000 inhabitants had perished from the combined toll of disease and socio-political turmoil.

The historical narrative surrounding Pẹhẹ serves as a poignant reminder of the critical importance of vigilance in public health. As modern societies face similar challenges, the lessons from the past underscore the risk of complacency in managing health crises. The tragedy of Ọyọ-Ilé during this period illustrates how the mismanagement of a public health emergency can have lasting impacts on a nation’s socio-political fabric, potentially leading to its downfall. This ongoing relevance is particularly critical in light of contemporary health challenges, such as the recent Ebola case reported in Abuja, urging authorities to respond without delay.
