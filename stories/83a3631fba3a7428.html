FG considers law to prevent reversal of economic reforms

2026-10-26

The Federal Government is considering legislation to protect economic reforms from reversal by future administrations. Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele announced the proposal at the 32nd Nigerian Economic Summit in Abuja, saying the government was seeking national agreement on principles to guide decisions on taxation, tariffs, subsidies, investment and industrial development.

The proposed Nigeria Economic Policy Framework is still a consultation draft and has not been approved as government policy. Public consultation is due to continue until November 27, 2026, after which the document is to go to the Federal Executive Council for approval and the National Economic Council for endorsement. Oyedele said the framework could also guide public officials and political appointees, potentially forming part of screening for government appointments and accompanying appointment letters.

The draft proposes mandatory Policy Impact Assessments before major economic interventions, requiring regulators to set out evidence, costs, beneficiaries and expected duration. It also recommends an annual National Economic Scorecard, published by Independence Day, to track real wages, productive employment and poverty. The framework’s stated pillars include stability, productivity, enterprise, capital, people, markets and institutions, with private businesses expected to drive growth under predictable rules.

Oyedele argued that policy uncertainty discourages investment and said future administrations should not abandon reforms for short-term political reasons, particularly the removal of petrol subsidies. He also called for temporary, performance-based protection for domestic industries, alongside stronger electricity, logistics and manufacturing competitiveness. The minister said the government was targeting low single-digit inflation before the end of 2027, while declining to set a firm timetable for lower borrowing costs.

Other summit speakers stressed the need to sustain macroeconomic stability while ensuring reforms produce jobs and improvements in household welfare. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo said growth of about four per cent was insufficient for rapid poverty reduction and employment creation. Participants also highlighted infrastructure, electricity, logistics and export development as priorities for raising productivity and competitiveness.
