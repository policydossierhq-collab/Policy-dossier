Yobe activates emergency response over Ebola, says state has no cases

Yobe State has activated its public health emergency response system after Nigeria confirmed an imported case of Ebola virus disease. At a briefing in Damaturu on Monday, Permanent Secretary of the Ministry of Health and Human Services Hadiza Sabo said no cases had been recorded in Yobe.

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention confirmed the case on October 20, 2026. Sabo said the patient is a diplomat who travelled from Uganda and tested positive for the Bundibugyo strain. The patient is in isolation and receiving care, while the NCDC has activated its national incident management system. As of October 24, 53 close contacts, including healthcare workers, patients, caregivers and travel-related contacts, were being monitored. Forty-six exposed people had also been placed on a 21-day no-fly list as a precaution.

Sabo said the NCDC had assessed the risk of Ebola importation as high, citing regional transmission, international travel and porous borders. However, she said there was no evidence of community transmission in Nigeria. Governor Mai Mala Buni has directed the activation of Yobe’s emergency incident management system and the preparation of a state response plan, she added.

The state has put its Emergency Operations Centre on high alert, increased disease surveillance across all 17 local government areas and strengthened contact tracing. Designated isolation and treatment facilities in the state’s three senatorial zones have been equipped with medicines and personal protective equipment. Health workers have also received further training, and the state is coordinating with the NCDC, the World Health Organisation and UNICEF.

Sabo urged residents to wash their hands regularly, avoid contact with the bodily fluids of sick people and avoid burial practices involving direct contact with corpses. She also advised people to rely on official updates, avoid spreading unverified information and not stigmatise anyone identified as a contact. Being listed as a contact does not mean a person has contracted Ebola, she said.
