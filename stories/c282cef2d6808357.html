‘Debt worse than leprosy’ – Obasanjo tackles Tinubu

Former President Olusegun Obasanjo criticised President Bola Tinubu’s defence of government borrowing, saying accumulated debt could burden generations to come. Speaking Friday at a public forum in Abeokuta, Ogun State, he argued that debt’s effects extend beyond individuals and can mortgage a country’s future. Tinubu said in April that borrowing was not inherently wrong if Nigeria could repay it. The debate comes amid concerns over the country’s rising debt and the share of government revenue used for servicing it.

Obasanjo also questioned Nigeria’s economic record, citing the naira’s decline against the US dollar. He recalled that when he established his farm 47 years ago, one naira was worth $1.85, and contrasted that with a rate of 1,500 naira to the dollar. He urged political leaders to show they understand the country’s problems and can identify practical responses. He also said Nigeria should scrutinise prescriptions from the World Bank and International Monetary Fund rather than assume they suit the country’s circumstances.

On artificial intelligence, Obasanjo warned that African countries could face a new form of external exploitation if foreign interests control the technology. Though he said he lacked technical expertise in AI, he argued that technologies beyond a country’s control could be used against it. He urged leaders to examine what technology transfer means and whether its terms serve African interests. Obasanjo also pointed to the estimated 24 million Nigerian children out of school and called for AI to help improve access to education. In agriculture, he said the technology could help farmers select suitable crops, raise productivity and support food security.

Historian Umar Ardo, the event’s guest speaker, warned that AI could deepen inequality if African countries remain dependent on foreign technology firms. He called for stronger protection of data and traditional knowledge, domestic computing and energy capacity, investment in local AI research, and models suited to African languages and practices. Ardo also urged governments to direct AI applications towards services such as primary healthcare and agricultural support, and called for cooperation among African countries on data rules and shared infrastructure. He said the continent should seek ownership and a fair share of the value generated from its data, rather than remain a consumer of technology developed elsewhere.
