Why I abandoned law for acting – Femi Adebayo

2026-11-14

Nollywood actor and filmmaker Femi Adebayo says he left legal practice in 2005 to pursue acting and film production, drawn by the work’s financial rewards and the greater fulfilment it brought him. He discussed the decision in an interview on the Afrobeats podcast, published on Saturday, reflecting on his career and his upbringing in an entertainment family.

Adebayo was called to the Nigerian Bar in 2003 and spent about two years practising litigation. He said he appeared in court and handled cases before deciding to leave law. Acting, which began as a hobby, had become the work he felt more passionate about, he explained, and he found it made him happier than the career he had studied for.

He said his father, veteran actor Adebayo Salami, known as Oga Bello, initially welcomed his choice to study law. Salami had wanted to become a lawyer himself but, according to his son, lacked the financial resources to pursue legal studies. Adebayo said his father’s reaction to his eventual departure from law was mixed: he was glad his son was following him into acting, but had also hoped to see him continue in the profession he had once wanted for himself.

Adebayo said his father later became grateful for the path he chose. His work has since extended across several parts of filmmaking, including acting, producing, writing and directing. His account describes a transition from a short period in litigation to a full-time career in entertainment, shaped by both personal interest and the practical rewards he saw in the industry.
