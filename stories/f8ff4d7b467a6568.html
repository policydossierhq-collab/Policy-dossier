Tinubu’s five-year defence plan ready to tackle insecurity – APC Campaign Council

2026-11-15

The All Progressives Congress Presidential Campaign Council says President Bola Tinubu’s proposed Five-Year Strategic Defence Operations Plan is ready. In a statement issued Sunday, spokesperson Dele Alake described it as a framework for coordinating defence priorities, operational needs, capability development and spending in response to current and anticipated security threats.

The council said the plan is intended to support a more coordinated, intelligence-led and technology-enabled approach to security, combining prevention with military operations. It reported that operations over the administration’s last three years had eliminated more than 15,000 terrorists and bandits. It also said more than 130,000 Boko Haram fighters and their dependants had surrendered through Operation Safe Corridor, the government’s rehabilitation programme for repentant insurgents. The council presented the figures as evidence of a strategy combining military action with non-military measures.

The statement said Tinubu had approved four new Army divisions and changes to existing formations to broaden operational coverage. It also cited approval for two Army depots, in Osogbo, Osun State, and Amasiri-Edda, Ebonyi State. Each facility can train 6,000 recruits at a time, according to the council; 5,210 recruits are currently training in Osogbo. The council said the depots are expected to speed up recruitment and increase the number of soldiers available for deployment. It added that the President had approved 28,000 additional soldiers and 50,000 additional police officers.

The council also pointed to investment in military accommodation and operational facilities, including a 700-unit barracks project in Ikere-Ekiti, Ekiti State. It said the plan would guide security-sector investment according to identified threats and national priorities, strengthen readiness and address capability gaps while making better use of available resources. The proposed framework forms part of what the council described as broader investment in personnel, equipment, technology, training and infrastructure.
