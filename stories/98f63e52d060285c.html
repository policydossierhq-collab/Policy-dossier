20:24 Inability To Transmit 2023 Presidential Election Results Eroded Trust In INEC — Ex-REC Igini

2026-10-25
Image: Channels TV

In a recent interview, former resident electoral commissioner Mike Igini expressed concerns regarding the trustworthiness of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) following the 2023 presidential election. Igini highlighted that the commission's inability to transmit results for this election, despite the functioning of the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS), has significantly eroded public confidence in its credibility.

Igini explained that while BVAS performed adequately during the elections, the failure to transmit the presidential election results, when results for the other two elections held on the same day were successfully sent, has led to a climate of distrust towards INEC. He indicated that this incident created a significant liability for the current leadership of the commission, stating, "What broke confidence...was the fact that there were three elections, same day; only one could not be transmitted, which was the presidential." He admitted that even he struggles to provide a clear explanation for the technical glitch that reportedly occurred.

Looking toward the future, Igini emphasized that the upcoming 2027 elections will serve as a crucial assessment of whether Nigerians can learn from the mistakes of the 2023 election. He characterized every election as a test, asserting that the next one will be pivotal for citizens to demonstrate their understanding and resolve regarding electoral integrity. "It will be a verdict on whether we, as a people, can learn from what has happened," he stated.

Furthermore, he urged Nigerian voters to remain vigilant during future elections, especially during the collation of votes, to ensure that their votes are protected. Igini reminded voters that their collective presence at polling units outweighs any attempts at compromise by electoral officials, reinforcing the notion that the power ultimately lies with the electorate. This situation underlines the ongoing challenges INEC faces in restoring public trust in the electoral process as Nigeria approaches its next round of elections.
