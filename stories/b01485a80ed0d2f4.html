2027: Violence, Intimidation, Use Of Thugs May Discourage Voters, Amupitan Warns Political Parties

2026-11-26

Independent National Electoral Commission Chairman Joash Amupitan has condemned violence linked to political campaigns, warning parties and their supporters that intimidation and the use of thugs could undermine the 2027 general election. He spoke on Thursday at a meeting between the commission and security agencies focused on strengthening election security ahead of the poll.

Amupitan urged political actors to campaign on issues, respect opposing views and stay within the law. He said political ambition should not come at the cost of citizens’ lives. Violence and intimidation recently reported during campaigns in some states, he warned, could discourage people from voting and weaken public confidence in the democratic process.

The INEC chairman said the commission was working with the Office of the National Security Adviser, led by Nuhu Ribadu, to identify potential flash points across the country. He described election security as a shared responsibility, requiring relevant agencies to work together to address violence, intimidation and other threats to credible elections.

Amupitan also called on security agencies to act professionally and impartially and to respect the rule of law. He warned that political interests must not interfere with their constitutional duties. Inspector-General of Police Tunji Disu, State Security Service Director-General Oluwatosin Ajayi and other officials attended the meeting.

In his welcoming address, Disu stressed the importance of stronger collaboration, intelligence sharing and coordinated security operations to respond to potential threats to the electoral process.
