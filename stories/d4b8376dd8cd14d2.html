Seyi Ebenezer: Meet the banker building a global payments business from Nigeria

2026-11-22

After 15 years in accounting and banking, Seyi Ebenezer turned from financing other businesses to building a payments company of his own. His career included roles at KPMG, Access Bank and Keystone Bank, where he worked with companies in sectors including energy, real estate, technology and logistics. A Lagos Business School profile said he handled more than $1 billion in debt and capital transactions. He holds an MBA from Lagos Business School, an Executive MBA from Northwestern University’s Kellogg School of Management and a finance certificate from Harvard University.

Ebenezer began building Payaza Africa Limited in 2021 while still at Keystone Bank, where he remained until February 2024. The company was incorporated on March 23, 2022, and began operations in July of that year. It provides digital payment processing and mobile payment services, helping businesses collect money and make disbursements through channels including cards and bank transfers.

The early years brought regulatory delays and substantial technology costs. In an October 2025 interview, Ebenezer said the company continued investing in technology while waiting for operating licences, before it was ready to sell its services. He said Payaza reached break-even one year and nine months after beginning operations. Its audited financial statements show revenue of N20.16 billion in 2024, up from N9.47 billion in 2023. Profit after tax rose from N3.30 billion to N7.27 billion over the same period.

Payaza has since pursued growth beyond Nigeria. A 2026 credit-rating assessment said its payout infrastructure supports more than 40 currencies, while a November 2026 report described the company as operating in 31 markets. Its international relationships include Visa and Mastercard. Payaza has also used Nigeria’s commercial paper market to fund its working capital: by February 2026, it was raising funds under a N150 billion programme and had raised more than N70 billion through six series, with N32.53 billion repaid. The company is also pursuing a listing on the Nasdaq Capital Market, according to the credit-rating assessment.
