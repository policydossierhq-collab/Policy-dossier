Investors need swift justice, not just incentives — Elumelu

2026-11-19

Tony Elumelu has urged President Bola Tinubu to make judicial reform a priority, arguing that economic incentives will not be enough to attract long-term investment if commercial disputes take too long to resolve or contracts are not reliably enforced. He made the call in Abuja on Thursday while delivering the keynote address at the WOC Justice Summit, themed “The Rule of Law as a Catalyst for Private Sector Growth and Economic Transformation.”

Elumelu said investors needed fair, impartial and timely court decisions, predictable regulations and protection for contractual agreements. He warned that doubts about enforcement could prompt capital to leave, and said contracts should remain enforceable through changes in government while regulations apply equally to businesses. He cited the World Bank’s Business Ready 2025 report, which gave Nigeria scores of 63.48 out of 100 for its commercial dispute resolution regulatory framework, 24.50 for public services and 53.80 for operational efficiency. The figures point to a gap between the rules and their delivery, as well as concerns about the effectiveness of commercial justice.

He commended Tinubu’s economic measures, including removing the petrol subsidy, unifying exchange rates and enacting tax reform laws, describing them as necessary responses to longstanding economic distortions. Elumelu also acknowledged the hardship these changes have placed on ordinary Nigerians. He argued that transparent regulation in the electricity sector could help draw investment and improve power supply for businesses and public institutions, but said court delays remained a barrier to private-sector growth.

Elumelu called for technology in the judiciary and measurable performance indicators for case management and dispute resolution. He said his own investments, including a 1997 acquisition of a struggling bank with other investors, depended in part on confidence that Nigerian law would protect transactions. He also called for Africa to move beyond exporting raw materials and importing finished goods, describing African businesses as central to economic transformation. The summit marked the 75th birthday and 50-year legal career of Senior Advocate of Nigeria Wole Olanipekun, whose legal career Elumelu praised.
