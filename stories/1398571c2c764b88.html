Nigeria’s remittance inflows hit $1.57 billion, May and June fuel record surge

2026-10-26

Nigeria received approximately $1.57 billion through International Money Transfer Operators (IMTOs) in the second quarter of 2026, setting a new high in the available quarterly series from 2019 to 2026. The total, covering April to June, was about 33% higher than the $1.18 billion recorded in the same period of 2025, according to the Central Bank of Nigeria’s Q2 2026 Statistical Bulletin.

May and June provided the main strength, with June posting the quarter’s highest monthly inflow. Their gains more than offset a year-on-year decline in April. The Q2 total also surpassed the previous quarterly record of about $1.47 billion, recorded in Q3 2024, and the prior second-quarter peak of approximately $1.26 billion in 2024.

Second-quarter inflows have varied over the years. They were about $551.86 million in 2019 and $482.58 million in 2020, then rose to $638.19 million in 2021 and $954.61 million in 2022. The total fell to $810.84 million in 2023, increased to $1.26 billion in 2024 and eased to $1.18 billion in 2025. The wider quarterly series also recorded about $1.40 billion in Q4 2025.

The second-quarter result followed inflows of $1.29 billion in Q1 2026, up 45% from $888.47 million a year earlier. Taken together, the figures point to stronger inflows in the first half of 2026 after receipts declined by 11.78% in the first half of 2025 compared with the same period in 2024. The rise comes amid CBN regulatory changes intended to strengthen oversight of international transfers and improve transparency in the foreign exchange market.

Source: Nairametrics
