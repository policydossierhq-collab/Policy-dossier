2027: No margin for error, INEC warns RECs, sets 6 operational priorities

2026-11-25

Independent National Electoral Commission Chairman Joash Amupitan has warned Resident Electoral Commissioners (RECs) that the 2027 general elections leave no margin for operational error. At a strategic meeting in Abuja, he set six priorities and called for prompt, documented action and personal accountability from the officials responsible for state-level preparations.

Amupitan said reviews of recent off-cycle polls—including governorship elections in Anambra, Ekiti and Osun, FCT Area Council elections and 16 legislative bye-elections—showed the value of early planning, orderly logistics and close supervision. He said problems identified in those exercises must be addressed before a nationwide simulation and ahead of the general elections. Presidential and National Assembly polls are scheduled for January 16, 2027, followed by governorship and State House of Assembly elections on February 6. INEC’s plans cover more than 100 million registered voters and 177,299 polling units, including provisions for 232,658 voters with disabilities.

The first priority is a mock accreditation and result-transmission exercise on November 28, 2026, in two polling units per ward. RECs are to oversee preparations and send INEC headquarters a technical report by November 30 covering network gaps, device faults and procedural obstacles. The second is last-mile logistics, including checks on transport arrangements and the readiness and security of Registration Area Centres. Third is enforcing a limit of 850 registered voters per Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) device, down from 1,250. Amupitan said INEC had acquired 57,000 additional devices and directed commissioners to ensure equipment is charged, configured, accounted for and backed up in high-density areas.

The fourth priority is extending the ICCES security framework to every local government area and ward, working with police, security officials and the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps. Election risk maps are to help identify potential flashpoints and resolve disputes over campaign rally locations or timing in advance. Fifth is recruiting, training and supporting more than 1.41 million temporary election workers, including National Youth Service Corps members, academics and civil servants. Amupitan stressed that their safety, competence and timely allowances matter to effective field operations.

The sixth priority is preventing information gaps, disinformation and partisan conduct. Amupitan urged regular engagement with political parties, observers, civil society groups and the media, and said staff who breach electoral rules or compromise neutrality would not be shielded. He also told RECs to supervise field audits themselves, report conditions candidly and stay in close contact with electoral officers so headquarters can respond to problems quickly.
