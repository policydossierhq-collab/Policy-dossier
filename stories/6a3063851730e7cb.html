Best performing money market funds in Nigeria as of October 2026

2026-11-13

First Ally Money Market Fund retained the top position in Nigeria’s money market fund rankings in October 2026, recording a year-to-date yield of 21.17%. The sector’s net asset value reached N6.90 trillion as of October 30, up 5.05% from N6.56 trillion in September. It comprised 50 funds following the addition of Custodian Money Market Fund and represented 68.31% of the N10.09 trillion mutual fund market.

DLM Money Market Fund placed second with a 20.47% yield, followed by Coronation at 20.31%, RT Briscoe Savings & Investment Fund at 20.19%, Page at 19.53% and Custodian at 19.52%. CardinalStone recorded 19.38%, Zedcrest 19.12%, Apel Wealth 18.98% and STL 18.93% to complete the top 10. STL narrowly held the final place, finishing 0.01 percentage point ahead of the fund ranked 11th, Comercio Partners.

Yields at the top of the table generally eased during the month: six of the seven funds that remained in the top 10 recorded lower year-to-date yields than in September. The top-10 entry threshold also declined, from 19.01% to 18.93%. CardinalStone was the exception, rising 0.07 percentage point to 19.38%. Custodian and Zedcrest entered the top 10, while Page returned after last appearing in August. First Ally’s yield fell from 21.43% in September, but it remained ahead of DLM by 0.70 percentage point.

Money market funds continue to attract retail and institutional investors seeking liquid options focused on capital preservation. Their returns remained attractive even as fixed-income yields moderated following the Central Bank of Nigeria’s September reduction of the Monetary Policy Rate from 26.5% to 23%. On the reported year-to-date figures, a hypothetical N5 million investment in First Ally at the start of the year would have generated about N1,058,500 in returns by October. Coronation was the largest fund in the top 10, with N126.28 billion in assets, while RT Briscoe was the smallest, with N736.06 million.

Source: Nairametrics
