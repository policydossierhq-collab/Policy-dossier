Ebola: Resident doctors demand urgent hospital preparedness, protection of frontline workers

2026-10-26

The Nigerian Association of Resident Doctors (NARD) has directed members nationwide to assess hospitals’ readiness to identify and manage suspected Ebola cases and ensure frontline staff have appropriate protective equipment. The instruction was issued in a circular dated 26 October, after Nigeria confirmed an imported Ebola case on 20 October.

NARD said resident doctors can face occupational exposure because they often assess patients whose febrile illnesses have not yet been diagnosed. It asked members to work with hospital management and infection prevention and control committees to review procedures for identifying, isolating and safely referring suspected cases.

The association also called for checks on the availability and accessibility of personal protective equipment, particularly in emergency, internal medicine, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, and other high-risk departments. Hospitals were asked to hold immediate staff sessions on NCDC case definitions, infection prevention, protective equipment use and occupational exposure protocols. Resident doctors should also know the reporting channels, designated surveillance officers and approved isolation and referral pathways.

Each centre is to appoint an Ebola preparedness focal person and submit a preliminary report to NARD’s national secretariat within 72 hours of receiving the circular. Reports should describe preparedness, available equipment, gaps and urgent needs. Members were also told to raise concerns about inadequate equipment, unsafe deployment or exposure through hospital safety structures. NARD’s national officers plan to engage the NCDC, the Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare and other stakeholders on training and occupational protection.

The case involved a 42-year-old woman who returned from Uganda via Addis Ababa on 3 October and later sought care at private facilities in Abuja. The NCDC was notified on 19 October, and tests confirmed Ebola the next day. The health minister said she was medically evacuated to Switzerland with her asymptomatic husband and was stable and clinically improved before the transfer. The WHO’s regional director for Africa subsequently said Nigeria had no active Ebola case, while stressing the importance of contact tracing and other public health measures.
