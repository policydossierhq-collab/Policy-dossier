Yoruba started home video, not Nollywood — Osuofia

2026-11-08

Veteran actor Nkem Owoh, known as Osuofia, has said Yoruba filmmakers helped popularise home-video productions in Nigeria but did not start Nollywood. He made the distinction during an interview with comedian and content creator Adebayo Abidemi, known as Bae U, after being asked about reports that he had credited Yoruba people with starting the industry.

Owoh said the name Nollywood was not in use when filmmakers began making movies. He described a process in which people discussed ideas together before eventually choosing the name. He rejected the claim that his position was that Yoruba filmmakers started Nollywood, while maintaining that they were early and influential in home video.

He cited veteran filmmaker Hubert Ogunde and Adebayo Papa as people whose work he admired, and said Yoruba productions helped make the home-video format popular. Owoh also disputed a suggestion that Ogunde was Igbo, saying the filmmaker was Yoruba.

The remarks come amid debate over the contributions of Yoruba and Igbo filmmakers to Nigerian cinema. The late actor Francis Duru had argued that Igbo entrepreneurs were central to building Nollywood, particularly through the growth of the commercial home-video market in the 1990s and early 2000s. Historical accounts also trace Yoruba filmmaking to travelling theatre groups, including those associated with Ogunde and Duro Ladipo, whose stage productions developed into film and video work before that commercial boom.

The 1992 Igbo-language film Living in Bondage, produced by Kenneth Nnebue, is associated with the rise of the modern direct-to-video market and helped popularise the format. The name Nollywood emerged later, as Nigerian films gained wider international recognition. Owoh’s comments underline the distinction between the earlier history of home-video production and the later industry identified by that name.
