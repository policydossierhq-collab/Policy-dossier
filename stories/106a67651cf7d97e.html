Beyond the battlefield: Crisis communication as a national security imperative, By Mukhtar Ya’u Madobi

2026-10-30

More than 150 representatives of security agencies, emergency services, government, academia and the media gathered in Kaduna for the Second National Symposium on Crisis Communication. Organised by the Centre for Crisis Communication on 27 and 28 October, the event focused on inter-agency cooperation, public trust and cyber threats. Participants argued that coordination between institutions must extend beyond operations to the information they release during emergencies.

Centre chairman Major General Chris Olukolade (rtd) warned that delays or conflicting official accounts can give rumours and manipulated claims room to spread. He cited figures indicating Nigeria had about 109 million internet users at the end of 2025 and estimated cybercrime losses of ₦1.1 trillion between 2017 and 2023. Brigadier General Murtala Abu, representing the Chief of Defence Staff, said cooperation in the field is incomplete without coordinated public communication. He also highlighted the risk that AI-generated audio, images and video could be used to deceive the public.

Participants raised concerns that deepfakes and organised disinformation could affect national security and public confidence, particularly ahead of the 2027 general elections. Kaduna State Commissioner for Information and Culture Ahmed Maiyaki, representing Governor Uba Sani, stressed the role of credible information in building trust. Speakers also recognised that some details may need to be withheld to protect operations, while warning that unexplained silence can fuel speculation.

The symposium’s communiqué proposed an Integrated National Crisis Information Architecture, supported by shared procedures for information exchange and public messaging. It also recommended tools to detect disinformation and deepfakes, improved digital literacy, and regulatory safeguards that protect security while respecting freedom of expression, fundamental rights and due process. The Centre unveiled the Nigerian Journal of Crisis Communication as a platform for research and practical guidance. Participants’ proposals, organisers said, will matter only if institutions put them into practice during future emergencies, sharing verified information promptly and communicating clearly without compromising sensitive operations.
