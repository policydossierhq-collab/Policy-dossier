Falana urges Nigerians to look beyond Abuja, hold states, LGs accountable

2026-10-30

Human rights lawyer Femi Falana has urged Nigerians to demand accountability from state and local governments, rather than focusing only on the federal government. He made the call in Lagos on Thursday at a screening of *LG Billions in the Dark*, a documentary examining local governance in Osun State. The film highlights a gap between allocations to the state’s 30 local government areas and the condition of roads, water, sanitation, healthcare and education in many communities.

Falana said the problems extend beyond Osun and are connected to weakened local councils and poor accountability. He argued that many residents have lost confidence in local government elections, which he said are often manipulated by ruling parties. He also criticised state authorities for retaining control over council finances despite the Supreme Court’s 11 July 2024 ruling that allocations for Nigeria’s 774 local governments should be paid directly to them from the federation account. Falana said the judgment has not been fully implemented in many states.

He urged residents to use the Freedom of Information Act to ask councils how much money they receive and how it is spent. University of Lagos Vice-Chancellor Folasade Ogunsola said local governments are vital to national development because they are closest to communities. She said councils should have a central role in urban planning and management, support local businesses and improve public spaces and essential services. Ogunsola described direct funding as a step in the right direction, while noting that challenges remain.

Participants in a panel discussion called for councils to publish budgets and spending online in accessible formats and to improve engagement with residents. They also urged greater participation by women, young people and people with disabilities, and recommended digital tools to monitor projects and track public programmes. The event included the unveiling of the Development Reporting Africa Foundation, the non-profit arm of DevReporting, which produced the documentary.
