Decoupling Sovereignty Index (5): How decoupling reinforces itself, By Max Amuchie

2026-11-01

The Decoupling Sovereignty Index (DSI) uses its Convergence Indicator to examine whether separate weaknesses in state authority are beginning to reinforce one another. The framework distinguishes a territory with several serious but contained problems from one where conditions across its Money, Land and Mind vectors combine to sustain an alternative order. Multiple high indicator scores alone are not enough to establish that such a system has formed; analysts must identify how the conditions interact and support that assessment with evidence and explicit rules.

The source describes a possible cycle in which an armed organisation’s economic extraction finances recruitment, weapons and territorial expansion. Control of territory can then allow the group to regulate movement, influence production and impose rules, creating further opportunities to extract resources. Residents’ compliance may reflect fear, dependence or a lack of alternatives rather than voluntary support, but it can still help the organisation maintain control. In this account, economic power, territorial authority and effects on public behaviour can reinforce one another.

The DSI calls a critical condition in which a Shadow Order is resilient and embedded enough to resist reversal through ordinary state intervention an Event Horizon. This is presented as an analytical concept, not a fixed threshold that applies identically everywhere. It does not mean recovery is impossible, nor does durability demonstrate popular legitimacy: coercion, economic dependence and the absence of credible alternatives may help sustain an order. The framework argues that addressing one symptom at a time may be inadequate when the underlying relationships remain intact.

Two designated indicator combinations illustrate the approach. M6 and L6 are associated with agricultural sovereignty collapse, where economic pressures and territorial insecurity can disrupt farming, transport and livelihoods. L7, L3 and MD5 are identified as a signal of human sovereignty collapse: conditions that constrain people’s ability to move, sustain ordinary life and exercise agency. The latter concerns people’s practical circumstances and does not replace or negate the state’s legal sovereignty. In both cases, the DSI’s focus is on the interaction among conditions, not simply on whether a particular problem exists.
