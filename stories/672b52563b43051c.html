Duchess Hospital marks five years of giant milestones

2026-10-26

Duchess International Hospital in Ikeja marked its fifth anniversary, with its management highlighting growth in specialist services and an effort to reduce the need for Nigerians to seek treatment abroad. The hospital said more Nigerians in the diaspora are returning for complex procedures, with patients able to avoid waiting lists overseas, reduce surgery costs by more than 50 per cent and recover near family and friends. It also said local patients are receiving care that might otherwise have led them to travel abroad.

Chief Operating Officer Dr. Olajide Ojo said the hospital treats an estimated 40,000 outpatients annually, delivers 500 babies and has carried out 2,053 paediatric and adult surgical procedures. About 65 per cent of those operations were major or complex, including spinal surgery, he said. Since launching its open-heart surgery programme in 2023, the hospital has performed 44 procedures: 20 on children and 24 on adults.

Ojo said a partnership with the Lagos State University Teaching Hospital, through the Duchess Waiting List Initiative, had helped improve access to cardiovascular surgery for patients facing long waits. Hospital chairman Dr. Yemi Onabowale pointed to its affordable-care programmes and community initiatives, and said the facility had received the Nigerian Healthcare Excellence Award for best private healthcare facility in Nigeria in 2024 and 2025. He said the hospital plans to expand access to care and strengthen specialist services, while investing in staff and technology.

Minister of State for Health Dr. Adekunle Salako said improving working conditions and remuneration, alongside establishing modern hospitals, was important to addressing the departure of medical professionals abroad. He commended the hospital’s facilities and patient care. Lagos State Governor’s wife Dr. Ibijoke Sanwo-Olu, represented by Ebute Metta General Hospital Medical Director Dr. Folakemi Irewole-Ojo, stressed the value of public-private partnerships in building capacity and improving services.

The anniversary lecture was delivered by cardiology professor Nicholas Ossei-Gerning of Spire Hospital in Cardiff. He discussed cardiovascular risk factors including high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, stress, depression, anxiety and lack of social support. He also described erectile dysfunction as a potential marker of cardiovascular disease and encouraged men to have regular health checks.
