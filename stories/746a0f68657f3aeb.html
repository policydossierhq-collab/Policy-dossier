South Africa cautions Fani-Kayode over social media remarks

2026-11-17

South Africa has conveyed its concerns to Nigeria’s High Commissioner, Femi Fani-Kayode, over events following the presentation of his diplomatic credentials and his subsequent social media remarks. The Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) said the concerns were raised during a meeting on Tuesday between its Director-General, Zane Dangor, and Fani-Kayode.

Dangor referred to Article 41 of the 1961 Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, which requires diplomatic agents to conduct official business with the receiving state through its foreign ministry. DIRCO said the meeting emphasised that official communications should use established diplomatic channels, including the department.

The dispute began after Fani-Kayode presented his Letters of Credence to President Cyril Ramaphosa at the Sefako Makgatho Presidential Guest House in Pretoria on November 13, alongside ambassadors from 14 other countries. Fani-Kayode alleged that the official livestream excluded his introduction and speech, saying it cut away when he was due to speak and resumed after his address. The South African Presidency later published a separate video of his speech.

Ramaphosa’s spokesperson, Vincent Magwenya, disputed Fani-Kayode’s account of the president’s response to his complaint. Magwenya said Ramaphosa had directed him to the appropriate officials and had no mandate to interfere in the South African Broadcasting Corporation’s operations or editorial decisions. The exchange prompted a public response from the Nigerian High Commission, which defended the envoy and called for restraint and resolution through diplomatic channels. The SABC said Fani-Kayode declined an interview invitation over the handling of the livestream, while remaining open to engagement with him and the mission.

DIRCO said South Africa remained committed to strengthening its strategic partnership with Nigeria and maintaining constructive engagement based on mutual respect and cooperation. The meeting marks a further effort to address the disagreement while preserving relations between the two countries.
