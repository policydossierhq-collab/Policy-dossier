Nigeria

Dockworkers threaten nationwide Port shutdown

2026-10-28

Dockworkers in Nigeria have threatened to shut down ports nationwide next week unless terminal operators begin negotiations on a new National Joint Industrial Council (NJIC) agreement. The Dockworkers Branch of the Maritime Workers’ Union of Nigeria (MWUN) said workers were increasingly frustrated by the delay and warned that port operations could be disrupted if talks did not start urgently.

Speaking in Lagos on Wednesday, branch president Bolaji Agboola said the previous NJIC agreement expired at the end of May 2026. Revised rates were expected to take effect from June 1, he said, adding that workers should already be receiving the updated rates.

Agboola said branch leaders were finding it increasingly difficult to contain workers’ agitation. He said they had continued engaging with dockworkers in an effort to prevent the dispute from escalating, but that frustration was growing as negotiations remained stalled.

The union branch called on the Federal Government to intervene through the Nigerian Maritime Administration and Safety Agency (NIMASA). Agboola urged the agency to ensure that terminal operators began negotiations before the close of work this week. He warned that port operations across the country could be disrupted next week if the dispute remained unresolved.

The dispute concerns a review of workers’ rates under the NJIC agreement. Agboola accused operators of terminals and jetties covered by the agreement of delaying discussions and urged them to begin negotiations without further delay.
