Does Nigeria truly have over 103 million voters?, By Chidi Anselm Odinkalu

2026-10-25

As Nigeria prepares for its general elections in January and February 2027, concerns mount regarding the integrity of its voter registration figures. While the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) asserts that there are over 103 million registered voters, analysts caution that this number may be inflated due to a lack of accurate demographic data. Reports indicate that voter turnout in the last election plummeted to a mere 26.7%, the lowest since Nigeria’s return to civilian rule in 1999, raising alarms about voter engagement and electoral integrity.

The historical context of voter turnout in Nigeria reveals a troubling trend. In 1999, the turnout was recorded at 52.3%, but this figure has steadily decreased over the years, with significant declines noted in successive elections. The turnout registered in 2023 represents a substantial decline from the 34.8% noted in 2019. Such trends indicate a growing disaffection among the electorate, compounded by complaints of 'voter apathy' prevalent in electoral discussions.

However, skepticism regarding the credibility of these numbers stems from the underlying issues within INEC's registration process. For instance, demographic records are flawed, with only 53% of births and a mere 10% of deaths officially recorded. This raises concerns over the accuracy of the electoral roll, leading to suspicions that many 'registered voters' could be deceased. INEC has previously struggled to update its register to reflect such realities due to the lack of reliable demographic data, emphasizing the urgent need for a proper demographic management system.

Further complicating the situation, Nigeria's population has more than doubled since 1999, yet the growth of the voter registration roll has not kept pace. This mismatch may signal potential systemic issues, including migration, civic disengagement, and administrative inefficiencies within INEC. A significant proportion of the current voter register might include deceased individuals, and there is a growing call for transparency and reliability in the electoral process to bolster public confidence.

As discussions around electoral reform intensify, it becomes increasingly clear that merely increasing the number of registered voters is insufficient without addressing the conditions that affect voter participation. With ongoing debates about voter integrity and electoral transparency, the upcoming elections will serve as a critical test for Nigeria’s democratic processes and for INEC's ability to manage a credible electoral register.
