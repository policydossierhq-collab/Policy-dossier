Niger village attack sparks deadly reprisals

2026-11-21

Suspected terrorists attacked Lumman-Ba’are village in Niger State’s Borgu Local Government Area at about 5 a.m. on Saturday, reportedly killing at least 11 people. The dead included the village head, his wife and children. Residents said the attackers also set houses ablaze, abducted people and stole cattle. The Niger State Police Command confirmed an attack in the village; its spokesperson said police had received a report that about five people were killed and that joint security teams were deployed to pursue the suspected attackers.

A separate attack was reported in Gada-Oli at about 2 p.m., while residents were observing afternoon prayers. The number of people killed there was not specified. Residents fled both communities, and reports said worshippers were fired on during morning prayers and the village head’s palace was burned.

The initial attacks were followed by violence against Fulani residents and people displaced to nearby communities. Eyewitnesses said Kambari militias carried out reprisal attacks in Wawa, Gada Oli, Tunga Ibrahim and other villages where Fulani people lived or had sought refuge. Two witnesses estimated that about 20 bodies were in Wawa, but the overall death toll from the reprisals remains unclear. Witnesses also said the attacks lasted roughly an hour and that security personnel were not present in Wawa. Sources linked the violence there to the withdrawal of mobile police officers who had been deployed about two months earlier.

The communities have faced rising tensions amid repeated attacks attributed to a Boko Haram offshoot. Some local residents have alleged that Fulani people assisted the insurgents, a claim that has intensified hostility between the groups. Kambari militias have previously targeted Fulani residents after attacks on villages including Pisa, Kaduna Daji and Kabe, forcing many to seek safety in towns and neighbouring Kebbi State. The latest killings add to a series of attacks on rural communities in Niger State, where previous raids have killed and abducted residents, destroyed property and driven people from their homes.
