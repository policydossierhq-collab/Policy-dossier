‘There’s Pain In My Mind And Soul,’ Says US Woman Who Survived Botched Execution

2026-10-30

Christa Pike says she has been in constant pain since surviving a failed execution attempt at a Tennessee prison a month ago. In a statement released Friday by her lawyers, the 50-year-old described ongoing physical pain and difficulty speaking, as well as emotional distress. “There is pain in my mind and in my soul,” she said.

Pike received two doses of pentobarbital on September 30, but the usually fatal amount did not kill her. She was taken to hospital in critical condition, received medical treatment and later returned to prison. The Death Penalty Information Center says she is the only inmate known to have survived after being injected with lethal doses of execution drugs.

Pike was sentenced to death for the killing of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer at a job training camp. Pike was 18 at the time; she and her boyfriend beat and killed Slemmer. Pike said she is recovering, while cautioning that healing does not mean she is okay. She said she does not know why she survived or what will happen next, and is trying to heal one day at a time.

Since the attempt, Pike’s lawyers have repeatedly asked Tennessee Governor Bill Lee to commute her sentence to life in prison. Lee has suspended executions in the state while an independent investigation is conducted. Asked about Pike’s fate this week, he said he expected her sentence to remain unchanged unless the courts determined otherwise.

There have been 32 executions in the United States this year, all by lethal injection. The death penalty has been abolished in 23 states, while California, Oregon and Pennsylvania have moratoriums in place.
