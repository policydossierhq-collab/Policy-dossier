IG proposes 200% gratuity and pension for retired police officers

2026-10-25

Inspector-General of Police Olatunji Disu has proposed a 200 per cent gratuity and pension package for retired police officers, as the Nigeria Police Force seeks to address longstanding pension challenges. Disu said in Abuja on Sunday that the proposal had been submitted to a Presidential Committee examining police concerns and possible solutions. He stressed that it was not an approved benefit or an existing entitlement.

Under the proposed framework, eligible officers’ pension benefits would be linked to their final salaries, alongside an enhanced gratuity. The committee includes representatives of the Ministry of Finance, the Office of the Accountant-General of the Federation, the National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission, the Budget Office of the Federation and the National Pension Commission.

The committee is expected to recommend measures covering outstanding pension liabilities, funding arrangements and benefit administration. Disu said the proposed changes were intended to improve retirement benefits and make pension administration more efficient. They are also expected to address recurring protests by serving officers seeking to leave the Contributory Pension Scheme, an issue that has remained contentious within the force.

Disu said the proposal remained subject to the committee’s deliberations, government approvals, funding arrangements and implementation guidelines. He added that President Bola Tinubu’s intervention in the pension issue had raised expectations among retired officers, who have complained about inadequate benefits, delayed payments and administrative bottlenecks. Disu said timely pension access was important for retirees facing household expenses and medical bills amid rising living costs.

According to the inspector-general, the Federal Government has cleared a backlog that had left some retirees waiting as long as two years for payments and introduced pension increases. He said some beneficiaries were now receiving almost twice their previous pension payments. The proposed changes come amid longstanding calls from retired officers for improved benefits and an exit from the contributory scheme.
