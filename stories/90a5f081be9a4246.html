Nigeria’s $1bn Monthly Remittance Has Been Achieved – CBN Governor Cardoso

2026-11-27

Nigeria has reached its target of $1 billion in monthly diaspora remittances, Central Bank of Nigeria Governor Olayemi Cardoso said at the 61st Annual Bankers’ Dinner of the Chartered Institute of Bankers of Nigeria in Lagos on Friday. The next goal is to raise monthly inflows to $1.5 billion by the end of the first half of 2027.

Cardoso commended licensed International Money Transfer Operators for strengthening transparency, investing in technology and expanding formal remittance channels, particularly in Asia. He also pointed to greater access to card payments abroad, saying the changes had brought more convenience and helped restore respect for Nigerians overseas.

He said the next phase of financial reforms should deepen liquidity, broaden participation and increase the financial instruments available to businesses and investors. The naira had strengthened by about 6 per cent over the past year, he said, while daily foreign-exchange market turnover had at times exceeded $1 billion. Fixed-income market turnover averaged N1.5 trillion a day, supported by a tradable benchmark yield curve extending to 10 years.

Cardoso described those indicators as signs of growing market confidence, while cautioning that functioning markets do not keep prices static. He said currencies, including the naira, can fluctuate in response to economic fundamentals and global developments, including changes in US interest rates and investor risk sentiment. Nigeria was better equipped to manage external shocks than it had been three years earlier, he said, citing stronger external buffers and the CBN’s readiness to use reserves when necessary to address disorderly market conditions.
