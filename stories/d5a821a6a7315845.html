Nigerians, others to face 10-yr wait for UK permanent residency

2026-11-02

The UK government has revised its proposed immigration reforms, keeping plans to extend the standard wait for permanent settlement from five years to 10. The changes are expected to take effect next year and will also apply to migrants already living and working in Britain. Permanent settlement is formally known as indefinite leave to remain.

Hundreds of thousands of care workers will be exempt from the longer wait and retain a five-year route, subject to eligibility requirements. The concession is relevant to Nigerians employed in Britain’s health and social care sector, which relies on overseas workers to help fill staffing shortages.

The government initially proposed that lower-skilled foreign workers wait between 10 and 15 years before qualifying. The revised plans followed opposition from trade unions and Labour Party members, who raised concerns about the effects on low-paid foreign workers, particularly in care jobs.

Under the new arrangements, migrants earning at least £50,271 a year and certain skilled public-sector workers can apply after five years. Those earning more than £125,000 may qualify after three years. Refugees who entered Britain illegally, including people who arrived by small boats, could face substantially longer waits, potentially lasting decades. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said the policy was intended to reward contributions to society while setting tougher conditions for people who break immigration rules.

The government estimates the reforms will reduce by 500,000 the number of migrants who arrived from 2021 and would otherwise have qualified for settlement by 2030. It still expects as many as 1.4 million migrants to qualify by then under the revised arrangements.
