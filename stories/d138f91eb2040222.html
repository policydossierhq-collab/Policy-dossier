Nigeria’s trade sector faces financing squeeze, profit margins slip – NESG

Limited access to financing was the most frequently cited constraint for businesses in Nigeria’s trade sector in the first half of 2026, according to a Nigerian Economic Summit Group report. The report examines operating conditions, business performance and expectations in the sector.

Financing ranked first among reported constraints, with a score of 12. Inadequate power supply, unclear economic policies, and the cost of commercial leases and rental property each scored 10. Financing rose from second place in 2024 to the top position, while foreign exchange availability became less prominent, which the report described as an encouraging development.

The Access to Credit Index stood at 112.0, and the Cash Flow Index was 121.9; both were in expansionary territory. However, the NESG said the availability of some credit does not guarantee businesses can secure funding at suitable costs, amounts or repayment periods to support expansion. The Operating Profit Index fell to 98.1 from 102.9 in the first half of 2025, indicating a marginal move into contraction.

The Future Investment Index was neutral at 100. Traders were optimistic about future turnover and operating conditions, but those expectations had not translated into a clear expansionary investment outlook. The report’s findings point to a gap between positive liquidity indicators and businesses’ ability to invest and sustain profitability.

The concerns come as merchandise trade grows. Nigeria’s total merchandise trade reached N41.44 trillion in the second quarter of 2026, up 5.61% from N39.24 trillion in the corresponding quarter of 2025. Trade contributed 17.89% to GDP in the first quarter of 2026. The report identifies financing access, infrastructure challenges and pressure on profits as continuing concerns despite the growth in trade activity.

Source: Nairametrics
