FBI offers $5,000 reward for wanted Nigerian woman

2026-10-31

The FBI is offering a reward of up to $5,000 for information leading to the arrest of Emuobosan Emanuella Hall, who failed to report to a federal prison to begin serving an eight-year sentence. Hall, 46, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit money laundering and conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud. She was sentenced to 96 months in prison in January 2026.

Hall was charged in 2024 over her role in a romance scam and was arrested in Georgia before being released on bond. The FBI says she did not appear at the facility where she was ordered to serve her sentence in March 2026. A federal arrest warrant was issued on March 31, and she was later charged with failing to appear for service of a sentence and indicted on that charge.

According to the US Department of Justice, people involved in the scheme approached victims on social media, presenting themselves as generals, philanthropists or entrepreneurs based outside the United States. After forming romantic relationships, they persuaded victims to send money under false pretences, including claims that funds were needed for charity or sick relatives. Hall and co-defendant Kenneth G. Akpieyi used Le Beau Monde LLC to handle money from victims. Hall admitted responsibility for $851,207 in losses. Akpieyi was held responsible for more than $3.5 million in losses and sentenced to 25 years in prison in 2025.

The FBI said Hall’s electronic monitoring device last transmitted from Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport on March 24, 2026. She had told her probation officer she planned to travel to Minnesota, where she was expected to serve her sentence, but airline records showed she did not board the scheduled flight. Hall was born in Nigeria and is also known as Emuobosan E. Hall, Emuobosan Emanuella Erih and “Mobo.” If convicted of failing to report, she could face up to 10 additional years in prison, served consecutively to her existing sentence, and a fine of up to $250,000. The FBI is asking anyone with information about her whereabouts to contact the agency.
