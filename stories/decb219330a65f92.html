The 2027 elections: Upsetting the applecart?, By Jibrin Ibrahim

2026-11-06

A Federal High Court in Abuja has ruled against the African Democratic Congress leadership led by former Senate President David Mark, invalidating decisions made by its caretaker committee and directing the Independent National Electoral Commission not to recognise the party’s 2027 election nominations. The ruling, delivered by Justice Peter Lifu, upheld a challenge by former ADC deputy national chairman Nafiu Bala Gombe, who argued that he was entitled to lead the party after its national chairman resigned. The decision affects the party’s national officers, including National Secretary Rauf Aregbesola, and candidates selected under the Mark-led committee, among them presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar.

Atiku condemned the judgment, describing it as an attempt to weaken the ADC and obstruct his candidacy. In a statement issued by his campaign’s strategic communications director, he pointed to a large turnout at an ADC event in Sokoto shortly before the ruling and said the campaign would continue. The ADC has also questioned why a second case addressed the leadership dispute after Justice Musa Liman of the same court dismissed an earlier challenge. That ruling cited jurisdiction, standing and failure to exhaust internal party remedies, and resolved the substantive issues in favour of the defendants.

The dispute has prompted wider concerns about whether opposition parties can compete freely in the 2027 elections. The article argues that reports of restrictions on campaign access, attacks on candidates and limits on access to media, billboards and venues contribute to an uneven political environment. These claims are presented as part of a broader concern about the use of incumbency and the conditions under which the elections will be conducted.

It also highlights electoral rules and administration. Section 60(3) of the Electoral Act provides for electronic transmission of polling-unit results to IReV while retaining signed physical Form EC8A as the basis for collation when transmission fails. The article says uncertainty about how failure is determined could create disputes. It further raises concerns that a polling-unit form used to document places where elections did not occur may have been dropped from the 2027 guidelines, and questions INEC’s restoration of more than 20 state assembly constituencies and creation of over 450 polling units without National Assembly approval or stakeholder involvement. The article concludes that close public scrutiny will be important to the credibility of the polls.
