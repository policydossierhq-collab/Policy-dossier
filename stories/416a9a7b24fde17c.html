Mexico captures drug kingpin on FBI’s most wanted list

2026-11-21

Mexico’s military said Saturday it had captured Fausto Isidro Meza, known as “Chapo Isidro,” during a special forces operation in Sinaloa state. The raid took place Friday night and also resulted in the capture of two other leaders of the group, including Meza’s second-in-command and a man the military identified as a financial operator.

A firefight broke out between military personnel and gang members in the town of Guasave during the operation. Local media reported that the clash caused panic in the surrounding area, including at a professional baseball game.

The military described Meza as the alleged leader of the Beltran Leyva gang, which it said is allied with one of two rival factions in the Sinaloa cartel. The three men were flown to Mexico City aboard a Mexican Air Force aircraft to be placed in the custody of prosecutorial authorities, the military said.

Meza faces criminal charges in Mexico and the United States. The FBI had included him on its list of 10 most-wanted fugitives and offered a $5 million reward for information leading to his arrest. An FBI bulletin, using the name Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, says his group is responsible for importing large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States.
