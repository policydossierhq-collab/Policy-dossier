CBD COP17: Nigeria, six others each win $50,000 grant for biodiversity projects

2026-10-25

Nigeria and six other countries have each received a $50,000 seed grant for projects intended to advance their 30×30 conservation targets. The awards were announced on the sidelines of the 17th session of the United Nations Convention on Biological Diversity in Armenia. The beneficiary countries are Costa Rica, Mexico, Nigeria, Nepal, Zambia, Rwanda and Kenya.

Nigeria’s grant will support a project on ecological connectivity, terrestrial and marine Other Effective Area-based Conservation Measures (OECMs), and protected-area management effectiveness. The Nigerian Conservation Foundation (NCF) will implement the project. Its director-general, Joseph Onoja, said the foundation would work with the Federal Ministry of Environment to provide scientific and technical support.

The project is expected to identify and map priority terrestrial and marine OECMs, assess how effectively protected areas are managed, and develop a national framework for evaluating management effectiveness. The work is intended to produce evidence and recommendations to strengthen Nigeria’s protected and conserved areas and support implementation of Target 3 of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. Nigeria’s biodiversity focal point, Sikeade Egbuwalo, said the funding could also help address fragmented habitats.

The other grants will support projects tailored to each country’s conservation priorities. They include improving visitor-management planning at Costa Rica’s Cocos Island National Park; developing underwater acoustic baselines for Mexico’s marine protected areas; recognising OECMs in Nepal; assessing Zambia’s Busanga Swamps; supporting community-based conservation and ecological connectivity in Rwanda; and advancing community-led marine conservation in Kenya.

The Rapid Deployment Mechanism, established by the High Ambition Coalition for Nature and People, provides small-scale funding intended to address financing gaps and help countries move early-stage conservation work forward. The organisers describe the grants as a way to bridge initial project needs and larger sources of funding. The 30×30 goal is to conserve at least 30 per cent of land and sea by 2030.
