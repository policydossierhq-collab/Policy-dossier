War on terror: Over 2,000 suspected terrorists facing prosecution — Tinubu

2026-11-17

Tinubu says more than 2,000 suspected terrorists are facing prosecution and more than 1,000 have been convicted, as Nigerian security forces intensify operations against armed groups. He said the country had killed or arrested four of eight identified senior terrorist leaders. The president’s remarks were delivered by National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu at the opening of the 2026 Chief of Defence Intelligence Annual Conference in Abuja.

Tinubu cited the elimination of senior Islamic State leader Abu Bilal al-Minuki in May 2026 and the arrests of Abu Barra of Ansaru and Mamuda of Mahmudawa in 2025 among the reported gains. He also referred to action against Ahmed Ohida’s ISWAP southern expansion network. The four leaders still being pursued, he said, are Ba Shuwa of ISWAP, Abdullahi Oro of IS-Sahel, Sadiku of JAS in the North Central region and Bakura Doro in the Lake Chad Basin. Tinubu added that security forces had killed senior bandit leader Halilu Sububu and other fighters.

The president said the government aims to turn operational gains into lasting security through intelligence-sharing and coordinated work among security agencies. He called for greater use of artificial intelligence, geospatial intelligence and unmanned surveillance, while stressing that AI-generated analysis requires human verification and that data protection and cybersecurity must be built into systems. He also said he had directed the preparation of a national threat assessment and a five-year strategic defence operations plan.

Tinubu reported that incidents of illegal oil bunkering and damage to oil and gas infrastructure in the Niger Delta fell from 32 in the first quarter of 2026 to 17 in the third, a reduction of 46.88 per cent. He said crude oil and condensate production averaged about 1.68 million barrels per day in August 2026. Defence Minister Christopher Musa emphasised the importance of timely intelligence and indigenous defence production, while Chief of Defence Staff Olufemi Oluyede called for more investment in intelligence technology and proactive operations.
