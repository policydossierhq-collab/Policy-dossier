Malaysia arrests 53 Nigerians over immigration offences – Report

2026-11-13

Malaysia arrests 53 Nigerians in immigration operations

Malaysia’s Immigration Department arrested 112 African nationals in enforcement operations conducted across the country between January and October 31, according to figures disclosed by department director-general Datuk Zakaria Shaaban. Nigerians made up the largest group, with 53 arrests, followed by 13 Ugandans, 10 Kenyans and eight Ghanaians.

The remaining arrests included six each from Somalia and Tanzania, four from Senegal, three from Algeria, and two each from Burkina Faso, Togo and The Gambia. One person each from Guinea, Zimbabwe and Libya was also detained.

Shaaban said the most common offence was entering or staying in Malaysia without a valid pass or permit, under Section 6(1)(c) of the Immigration Act 1959/63. That offence accounted for 85 arrests, including 47 Nigerians. A further 23 people were detained for overstaying under Section 15(1)(c), while three were arrested for breaching the conditions of their immigration passes under Regulation 39(b) of the Immigration Regulations 1963. One Nigerian was also arrested over alleged possession of forged documents under Section 56(1)(l) of the Act.

Authorities separately arrested an Algerian employer under Section 55B of the Act for allegedly employing foreign workers without valid passes. Shaaban said enforcement operations were intended to address undocumented migration and take action against employers who employ or accommodate foreign nationals without proper immigration documents. The report did not identify the Nigerians arrested or specify where they were apprehended.
