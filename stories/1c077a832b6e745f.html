FG clears pension backlog accumulated since 2013 – PTAD

2026-11-27

The Federal Government has cleared outstanding unfunded pension liabilities inherited by the Pension Transitional Arrangement Directorate (PTAD) since it began administering the Defined Benefit Scheme (DBS) in 2013, Executive Secretary Tolulope Odunaiya said at a stakeholders’ forum in Lagos. She said the budget line for those liabilities is expected to fall to zero in 2027, as no further funding will be needed to settle them under the scheme.

Odunaiya said the minimum monthly pension paid under the DBS had risen to N58,000 from N6,024 in 2024. She attributed the increase to pension increments and a harmonisation policy, which calculates entitlements using the salary structure applicable at the scheme’s 2007 cut-off date rather than the structure in place when an individual retired. PTAD said eligible pensioners had received all arrears arising from the harmonisation and other increases. Monthly pension disbursements have grown from about N8bn to more than N13bn, Odunaiya added.

The directorate is working with the National Health Insurance Authority on nationwide healthcare coverage for DBS pensioners, with implementation set to be prioritised in 2027. PTAD has also upgraded its verification system and Aliveness Confirmation Portal so pensioners can complete annual checks remotely using electronic devices. Officials demonstrated those tools, an integrated automation system and an online complaints portal at the forum.

Public Complaints Commission Chief Commissioner Bashir Abubakar, represented at the event by Deputy Director of Investigation Amusat Medinat, said PTAD had resolved about 80 per cent of pension-related cases referred by the commission. He said many unresolved cases involved missing documents or failure to follow instructions, and that a dedicated desk officer would coordinate with PTAD’s Lagos office.

The Nigeria Union of Pensioners welcomed the clearance of arrears and the planned health scheme, while urging PTAD to ensure pension increases remain part of regular payments. The union also called for monitoring to ensure security-related palliatives reach their intended recipients. PTAD said it would continue working to maintain regular payments and improve its services.
