DSS Intercepts Arms Shipment From Niger Republic In Sokoto, Arrests Female Courier

2026-11-11

The Department of State Services (DSS) intercepted a shipment of weapons and ammunition in Dawakawa, Goronyo Local Government Area of Sokoto State. The consignment comprised 21 AK-47 rifles, four PKT machine guns and 825 magazines. A security source said the weapons had been smuggled into Nigeria from the Niger Republic and were intercepted after surveillance and intelligence gathering on suspected terrorist networks. The source placed the operation less than 70 days before the 2027 general elections.

Separately, DSS operatives arrested Hafsat Umar in Talata Mafara Local Government Area of Zamfara State earlier in the week. She was allegedly carrying 12 grenades and two ammunition belts. According to the source, intelligence indicated she had travelled to Niger State to obtain ammunition for delivery to suspected bandit leader Saidu Marafa, who was described as based in Kani Kawa, Maradun Local Government Area. The source said Umar would be charged in court.

The source said increased interceptions had led criminal groups to try other ways of transporting illegal arms, including using women and camels. The DSS has reported female-courier arrests in Zamfara, Nasarawa, Ebonyi, Taraba, Kaduna and Plateau states. Cases cited include the recovery of 202 rounds of AK-47 ammunition and three empty magazines from Aisha Mohammed in Keffi, Nasarawa State, and the arrest of two suspected couriers in Ebonyi in September 2026, when operatives recovered 14 wraps of improvised explosive device detonators.

Other reported seizures included ammunition recovered from suspects in Nasarawa and Zamfara, and an AK-47 rifle, ammunition and magazines found with two women arrested on the Wukari-Jalingo Road in Taraba State. The DSS has also recorded convictions in terrorism-related cases involving female arms couriers: the Federal High Court in Abuja sentenced Halima Abdullahi, Safiya Salihu and Halima Umar to 20 years in prison each. The security source said the service would continue intelligence-led operations against illegal arms trafficking.
