Otti urges lawyers to defend rights of vulnerable Nigerians

2026-11-11

Abia State Governor Alex Otti has urged lawyers to extend legal services to people unable to afford representation, saying access to justice should take priority over financial and professional interests. He made the appeal in Umuahia while opening the 2026 Nigerian Bar Association Section on Public Interest and Development Law (NBA-SPIDEL) Annual Conference, themed “In Public Interest: Advancing Justice, Democracy and Development.”

Otti said lawyers had a responsibility to help people who lacked the means to defend their rights. He also said his administration had found people in correctional facilities who, in its view, had committed no offences warranting continued detention. He cited the case of a person allegedly detained for supporting an opposition party and said he had directed the Attorney-General and Commissioner for Justice to work with the Chief Judge on securing the release of such inmates. Otti warned against using the justice system to punish people for expressing opinions or posting on social media.

The governor said the state would soon commission 10 newly constructed courthouses. He said the facilities would have renewable energy systems, digitalised court records and broadband connectivity. He also urged legal practitioners to prepare for technological changes in their profession.

In his keynote address, former Vice President Yemi Osinbajo urged lawyers to go beyond litigation and legal interpretation by advancing public interest and helping ensure democracy benefits citizens. He identified high legal costs, delays in court proceedings, judicial corruption and impunity as barriers to justice, and called for reforms that make legal services more affordable and accessible. NBA President Oyinkansola Badejo-Okunsanya also called on the section to strengthen its work on justice, democracy and development. She urged it to develop an intervention addressing the treatment of children in police custody and detention facilities, following reported deaths in custody. She asked NBA-SPIDEL to work with relevant NBA branches and human rights organisations, identify priorities and responsibilities within 30 days, and submit a progress report within 90 days.
