2027: Sowore faults NELFUND, promises non-repayable grants

2026-11-20

African Action Congress presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore says he would abolish the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND) if elected and replace its student loan scheme with grants. Speaking during a live session on his Facebook page on Friday, he proposed payments of N100,000 per semester, saying students should be able to pursue their studies without taking on debt.

Sowore said the proposed grant would be N20,000 more per semester than the amount students currently receive through the loan arrangement. At two semesters a year, he said, students would receive N200,000 annually and N400,000 over a four-year programme. He described the payments as an investment in young Nigerians and said they would help students focus on their education.

He argued that graduates could struggle to repay loans when employment opportunities were limited. Drawing on his experience in the United States, where he said he spent 20 years, Sowore described student debt as a source of financial hardship. He also argued that borrowers were not repaying the loans currently provided in Nigeria, and said this was a reason to convert the support into grants.

Sowore also proposed scholarships for students with outstanding academic ability, including sponsorship to study abroad. He called for greater government investment in education, saying stronger support for schools was needed to reduce poverty and develop human capital. Recalling free education and community efforts to keep children in school during his childhood, he contrasted those experiences with what he described as a decline in the sector. He also cited the former standing of the University College Hospital in Ibadan and the presence of Indian and Ghanaian teachers in Nigerian secondary schools in the 1980s as examples of the country’s past capacity.
