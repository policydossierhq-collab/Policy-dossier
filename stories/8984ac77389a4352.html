FG reviews 932 laws, drops 269 obsolete enactments

2026-11-12

The Federal Government has completed a review of 932 federal enactments and 731 subsidiary legislations for a proposed 2025 edition of the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria. At the report’s presentation in Abuja, Attorney General of the Federation Lateef Fagbemi said 663 enactments and their relevant subsidiary legislation were identified for inclusion, while 269 were classified as obsolete, spent or repealed.

Committee chairman Olawale Fapohunda gave a slightly different breakdown: he said 664 enactments and accompanying subsidiary legislation had been cleared for inclusion, with 268 dropped. The figures were presented at the same event. The committee also submitted draft copies of the proposed edition, arranged in 20 volumes, to Fagbemi for consideration and further executive action.

The review follows the last official consolidation of federal laws in 2004. Fagbemi said the intervening years had brought legislative, constitutional, institutional and technological changes, as well as new laws and amendments, making an updated and accessible collection necessary. He said the report would be presented to President Bola Tinubu for transmission to the National Assembly for legislative consideration.

Fapohunda said the committee used 14 thematic working groups and digital collaboration tools to review and harmonise the legislation. Its work also identified matters needing further attention, including outdated statutory language, cross-references and chapter numbers, inconsistencies in the sequencing of Acts, and fines and fees that no longer reflect current economic conditions. The committee recommended guidance for ministries, departments and agencies on reviewing their laws, a centralised repository of federal legislation, and a continuing process for updating the laws so another lengthy gap between consolidations can be avoided.
