ABBACARE: How Kano is expanding healthcare access for the most vulnerable

Kano State’s ABBACARE Initiative provides social health insurance coverage for vulnerable groups, including pregnant women, people living with sickle cell disorder and emergency patients. Implemented by the Kano State Contributory Healthcare Management Agency (KSCHMA), the programme aims to reduce the cost barriers that can prevent people from obtaining essential care.

Figures provided for the initiative show that 28,721 people have enrolled and that services are available through 13 hospitals across the state’s three senatorial zones. The numbers indicate the reach of the programme, but do not by themselves establish whether beneficiaries consistently receive timely, quality care. That will also depend on participating hospitals’ capacity and the availability of funding to sustain coverage.

ABBACARE is among the health interventions under Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf’s administration. Another, the Gida-Gida Lafiya Project, addresses hypertension and other non-communicable diseases by distributing customised digital blood pressure monitors. The devices are intended to help beneficiaries monitor their blood pressure, identify possible risks and seek medical attention when needed. Guidance in local languages is intended to make health information more accessible in communities.

The initiatives address different but related concerns: ABBACARE is intended to ease the financial burden of treatment, while Gida-Gida Lafiya promotes monitoring and early intervention. For ABBACARE, the next steps include maintaining coordination with hospitals, monitoring service delivery and ensuring transparent management of funds. The experiences of beneficiaries, particularly those relying on the scheme for maternal care, sickle cell treatment or emergency services, will be important in assessing its performance.

State-level schemes such as ABBACARE can contribute to broader efforts to advance Universal Health Coverage, but their wider value depends on evidence of outcomes, affordability and sustainability. For Kano, progress will be measured not only by enrolment, but by whether people can obtain reliable, affordable and quality care when they need it.
