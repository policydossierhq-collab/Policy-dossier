NANS threatens to shut down university over SUG president’s arrest

2026-11-24

The National Association of Nigerian Students (NANS) has threatened to mobilise students to shut down Osun State University (UNIOSUN) and occupy the Osun State Government Secretariat over the arrest and prosecution of the university’s Students’ Union Government president. NANS National President Akinteye Afeez announced the proposed action in a statement, alleging that Vice-Chancellor Clement Adebooye ordered the student leader’s arrest.

Afeez said NANS had instructed its zonal coordinator and the Osun State Joint Campus Committee chairman to organise the shutdown and a peaceful occupation of the secretariat. He called for Adebooye’s immediate removal and said the association’s national structures had been activated to relocate to Osun State until the matter was resolved. The statement did not give a date for the planned action or specify the alleged offence for which the student leader was charged.

The university has offered a different account. It said SUG activities had been suspended following an alleged assault on first-year student Emmanuel Onifade by union executives. The university identified the officials as SUG president Layonu Akintayo, assistant general secretary Adesanmi Tijesunimi and faculty chairman Oyedeji Samsideen. It alleged that Onifade had used the university’s logo and the Office of Student Affairs as the identity of a WhatsApp platform, and had threatened another student over a social media video concerning UNIOSUN students.

According to the university, the SUG president asked Onifade to meet at the main campus after the other student raised the matter with union executives. The university alleged that Onifade was then taken in a Students’ Union vehicle to a mechanic workshop in Osogbo, where he was beaten, and was assaulted again at another location. He was reportedly released with serious injuries, after which his father reported the matter to police. The university said the three executives were handed over to police and charged before a magistrate’s court in Oke-Fia. They were granted administrative bail, and the case was adjourned until 23 December. The suspension of SUG activities will remain in place while investigations continue, the university said.
