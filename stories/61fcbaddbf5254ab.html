Bayelsa Boat Tragedy: Death toll rises to 26

2026-11-21

The death toll from a boat accident in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State has risen to 26, as search and recovery operations continued into Sunday. Governor Douye Diri and his deputy visited the scene on Saturday to assess the response, while grieving relatives gathered at the Ayama waterfront.

The wooden passenger boat was travelling from Ayama to Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it capsized at about 11 a.m. on Friday, on the waterway between Oweikorogha and Otuan. The vessel was reportedly overcrowded and developed a mechanical fault during the journey.

The police initially reported four bodies recovered and 24 survivors rescued on Friday. By Saturday night, local divers and marine police had recovered 22 more bodies, bringing the death toll to 26. Nineteen survivors received treatment at Ayama Clinic, while five injured passengers were transferred to a hospital in Yenagoa. Recovery efforts were due to resume on Sunday, with divers also setting nets along the Oporoma waterways to catch bodies that may have drifted away.

A source close to the Southern Ijaw council chairman said the boat was old and not seaworthy, and that passengers had not been provided with life vests despite previous safety warnings. The council plans to enforce an order to apprehend and prosecute people who breach safety laws. The vessel’s manager was taken into custody for investigation, while the operator reportedly fled.

The Bayelsa government said it was supporting search-and-rescue operations and would assist emergency responders until they concluded. It warned operators against overloading, reckless navigation, speeding, travelling without life jackets, night journeys and operating in unfavourable weather. Community leaders have called for stricter checks on vessel seaworthiness and compliance with life-vest requirements.
