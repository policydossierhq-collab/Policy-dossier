Who Gets To Compete?

As campaigns gather pace and opposition figures discuss cooperation, questions about access, legal certainty and confidence in the vote are becoming central to the contest. Recent events have ranged from Peter Obi’s visit to a community affected by violence to an APC mobilisation rally and public questions about the electoral commission’s preparations. Together, they highlight that competing for votes also depends on candidates being able to organise and citizens being able to participate safely.

A reported attack on an NDC town hall gathering in Oregun, Lagos, left participants injured, according to reporting from the scene, and a suspect was arrested. The NDC alleged political responsibility, while the Lagos APC denied sponsoring the incident. The available reporting did not establish independent evidence linking the attackers to a political party. An investigation must determine who was responsible; meanwhile, political meetings should be accessible without fear of assault. Intimidation can deter attendees, volunteers and venues, affecting the conditions in which candidates compete.

Oyo Governor Seyi Makinde confirmed active discussions involving Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi, saying any decision should consider evidence, manifestos, past performance and public sentiment. He did not announce an agreement or endorse a candidate. Separately, INEC chairman Joash Amupitan said the commission would comply with binding court decisions concerning Atiku’s participation and noted that it faces more than 300 pending cases. The ADC says it has appealed a November 5 ruling and sought a stay of execution; the material available does not establish that a stay has been granted. The dispute remains unresolved in the information provided.

Obi visited people affected by the June 2025 attack in Yelwata, announced support for orphaned children’s education and a borehole, and held a town hall in Makurdi with running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso. In Akure, Vice-President Kashim Shettima represented President Bola Tinubu at an APC rally, calling for unity and mobilisation. The campaign set a target of 1.2 million votes in Ondo, an aspiration rather than a forecast. Rally activity and organisation do not establish how voters will decide.

INEC has announced a nationwide mock accreditation exercise for November 28 to test accreditation and electronic result transmission. Amupitan said the commission had procured additional BVAS devices and reduced the maximum number of voters assigned to each machine from 1,250 to 850. The exercise’s effectiveness remains to be tested; transparent findings on performance, problems and corrective steps would help voters assess the commission’s assurances. No fresh, credible polling in the material reviewed establishes a national shift. The standard for the coming weeks is whether lawful contenders can be heard, citizens can participate without intimidation and votes can be counted credibly.
