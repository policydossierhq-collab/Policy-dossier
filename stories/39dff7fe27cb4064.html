Tinubu mourns Okonjo-Iweala’s mother

2026-10-25

President Bola Tinubu has extended his heartfelt condolences to the Okonjo royal family of Ogwashi-Uku in Delta State on the passing of Professor Kamene Okonjo, who was the mother of Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, the Director-General of the World Trade Organization. Kamene Okonjo passed away at the age of 96. In a statement released by his Special Adviser on Information and Strategy, Bayo Onanuga, Tinubu hailed the late scholar as a champion of women’s empowerment and a revered matriarch in her community.

In his statement, President Tinubu described her legacy as one of intellectual distinction and service to humanity, highlighting her commitment to advancing women and society at large. He expressed his sorrow over her death, emphasizing the loss not only to her family but also to the community of Ogwashi-Uku, where she was known as a beloved mother, matriarch, and community leader.

Kamene Okonjo was a distinguished sociologist recognized for her significant contributions to the understanding of the political and social roles of Igbo women, particularly west of the River Niger. Her influential works, such as the essay "The Dual-Sex Political System in Operation: Igbo Women and Community Politics in Midwestern Nigeria" published in 1976, remains widely referenced within African gender studies. Additionally, her 1980 work, "The Role of Women in Social Change among the Igbo of Southeastern Nigeria Living West of the River Niger," further exemplifies her scholarly impact.

She was married to Chukwuka Okonjo, who served as both a mathematician and economist, eventually becoming the Obi of Ogwashi-Uku in 2007 before his passing in 2019. The couple had seven children, among whom Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala is the most prominent, having served as Nigeria’s Minister of Finance and as the former Managing Director of the World Bank.

Notably, Kamene Okonjo was also a victim of a high-profile kidnapping in 2012, a harrowing event linked to political tensions during her daughter's tenure in government. President Tinubu's heartfelt remarks serve to honor her legacy, and he prayed for her eternal rest while offering comfort to the grieving family and community.
