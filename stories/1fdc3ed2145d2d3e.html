US reports highest number of measles cases in 35 years

2026-11-06

The United States has recorded 5,182 confirmed measles cases so far in 2026, according to figures released by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The total is the highest the agency has recorded since 1991, and more than twice the 2,289 cases reported during all of 2025. Measles is highly contagious but preventable through vaccination.

State health officials have reported at least eight deaths linked to measles, including deaths among very young children. The disease can cause fever, respiratory symptoms and a rash. In some cases, it leads to serious complications, including pneumonia and inflammation of the brain, which can cause lasting health problems or death.

Measles was declared eradicated in the United States in 2000, following the introduction of vaccination. The disease has returned in recent years amid falling vaccination rates and growing public distrust of health authorities. Health Secretary Robert Kennedy Jr., who is known for skepticism about vaccines, has been accused of contributing to that distrust by spreading misinformation about the measles vaccine and playing down the risks of the disease.

Before a vaccine was developed in the early 1960s, measles killed hundreds of children in the United States each year. The disease continues to cause tens of thousands of deaths worldwide.
