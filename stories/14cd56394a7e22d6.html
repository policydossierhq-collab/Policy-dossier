US stocks: How to invest in the world’s largest stock market from Nigeria

2026-10-31

Nigerians can invest in US-listed companies and funds through local fintech apps or international brokerage accounts. The US market represents about 60% of global equity-market value. Its appeal includes access to major technology companies and index funds tracking markets such as the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100, which are not listed on the Nigerian Stock Exchange. US equities have also recorded average annualized S&P 500 total returns of roughly 10% to 10.5% since 1928, including reinvested dividends; after US inflation, the long-term average has been about 7%. Past performance does not establish future returns.

Local platforms such as Bamboo and RiseVest allow residents to buy shares or exchange-traded funds. Investors can fund accounts using locally accepted bank transfers or debit cards in naira, which is converted to dollars at the prevailing rate. Conversion spreads can reduce the amount invested. Exchange-rate movements also affect returns when proceeds are converted back to naira: a stronger naira can lower the local-currency value of an investment even if its dollar value has risen. Investors with domiciliary accounts or virtual US-dollar cards may fund accounts in dollars, though currency movements still matter when converting money back.

International brokers named as options include Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab International, Saxo Bank, Exante and Swissquote. These accounts are typically funded through foreign-currency wire transfers from local or offshore domiciliary accounts. They may suit high-net-worth investors seeking access to a wider range of investments and trading tools, but some services require larger sums. The source material notes that Robinhood, Fidelity and Vanguard generally require US tax residency or a Social Security number and do not support native non-resident accounts from Nigeria.

Taxes and costs are also part of the calculation. US dividends paid to non-US residents are subject to automatic 30% withholding, while gains from share sales are taxable under Nigeria’s personal income tax system, subject to exemptions for earnings below basic income levels. Investors can compare platforms’ foreign-exchange spreads and trading commissions. Rather than selecting individual companies, they may consider broad-market ETFs, such as funds tracking the S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100. A dollar-cost averaging approach—investing a fixed amount regularly regardless of price—is another strategy described in the source material.
