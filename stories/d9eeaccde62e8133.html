Pope Leo makes history with US midterm vote

2026-10-30

Pope Leo XIV has submitted a mail-in ballot in Chicago for the US midterm elections, marking the first known time a pope has voted in a political election. The Vatican said Friday that Leo, the first US-born pontiff, voted as a US citizen and supports citizens’ right and duty to take part in democratic processes. The midterms are scheduled for November 3.

The Vatican also confirmed that Leo voted when eligible during his years living in Peru. The pope, born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago, holds both US and Peruvian citizenship. Before his election as pontiff, he voted in Illinois primary elections, choosing Democratic ballots in 2008 and 2010 and Republican ballots in three later primaries, according to state and local records.

Leo’s vote comes amid a public rift with US President Donald Trump. The two have clashed over the administration’s immigration crackdown and the war on Iran. Trump has criticised the pope’s positions on crime and foreign policy, while Leo has said he is not afraid of the president.

In April, after Trump threatened to destroy Iran, Leo urged people to press political leaders to pursue peace and reject war and violence. In September, he also called on the faithful not to remain passive or indifferent toward current events, urging them to listen, consider the challenges and become involved. His vote is consistent with that public call for civic participation.
