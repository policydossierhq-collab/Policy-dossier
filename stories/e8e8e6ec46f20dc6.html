How I spent years in asylum camp before success in Europe – Nigerian bizman

2026-11-21

Jeffrey Ekhator, founder and group chief executive of Jeffangs Global Trade and Investment, says his business life began in childhood, selling goods for his mother in Benin City, Edo State. After secondary school, he worked at a filling station and later left Nigeria for France during the June 12 crisis. He moved on to the Netherlands, where he applied for political asylum because he could not study without legal documentation. He says he spent years in an asylum camp before receiving permission to stay.

Ekhator then attended integration school, college and a two-year car business programme. He says he could not complete the programme’s diploma requirements because he was unable to secure an internship. While studying at university, he worked evenings at McDonald’s and, in 2004, opened an African goods shop with his wife. The shop was destroyed by a fire just before its planned opening. Ekhator says authorities investigated but did not have enough evidence to make an arrest. He viewed the incident, and the lack of insurance compensation, as part of the discrimination he faced as a Black business owner.

After losing his investment and owing suppliers, Ekhator continued working and saving. He says an older Moroccan shopkeeper he met in Rotterdam offered to sell him a store for €25,000, payable in monthly instalments. Ekhator accepted the terms, refitted the shop with African products and drinks, and repaid the debt within a year. He later expanded into a second shop, a nightclub, a restaurant, a supermarket and a salon. His businesses import goods from several countries, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Uganda, the United States, the United Kingdom and China.

Ekhator also entered local politics, contesting a municipal election in 2022. He says he won but gave up his position in favour of a Ghanaian woman who had placed sixth. He described the campaign as a way to encourage Nigerians and other Africans to take part in local decision-making. Looking back on his progress, he points to focus, honesty and hard work as principles that have sustained him.
