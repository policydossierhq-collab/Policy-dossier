Invest In Training, Boost Production To 3m/b Per Day, PETAN Urges Indigenous Oil Firms

2026-10-26

The Petroleum Technology Association of Nigeria (PETAN) has called on indigenous oil and gas companies to invest in training young Nigerians, saying stronger technical capacity is needed to help reach the country’s target of producing three million barrels of crude oil per day. Chairman Wole Ogunsanya made the appeal at the association’s media roundtable in Lagos, pointing to a widening gap between experienced industry workers and younger professionals entering the sector.

Ogunsanya said many Nigerian professionals gained technical experience through international oil companies, but the transfer of onshore, swamp and shallow-water assets to local operators raises questions about how the next generation will be trained. He said Nigeria has educated graduates who could enter the industry, but they need encouragement and practical preparation in areas including drilling, pipeline installation, fabrication, welding and offshore operations. PETAN recruits nearly 100 engineers every two years through an internship programme, he said, with member companies employing more than 60 per cent of participants after their training. The association is also working with the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board on human-capacity initiatives and plans to establish a training centre in Port Harcourt, with facilities including drilling rigs.

The chairman warned that experienced Nigerian workers are increasingly being recruited by countries including Qatar, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, adding to the urgency of building local expertise. He also backed the Federal Government’s drill-or-drop policy, which requires operators to develop oil assets or risk losing them. Ogunsanya argued that developing existing discoveries could add output sooner than relying only on new exploration, which requires substantial investment and carries geological risks. More drilling and field activity, he said, would also create work for indigenous service companies, which may have equipment and signed contracts but earn little when operators do not begin projects.

Ogunsanya called for greater investment in gas production and distribution infrastructure, citing Nigeria’s estimated 210 trillion cubic feet of gas reserves. He said expanding gas supply and pipelines was necessary to support electricity generation and industry, noting that installed generation capacity is about 12,000 megawatts. He also supported reviewing aspects of the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act, arguing that industry developments had created areas requiring attention. He urged operators, regulators and government to address constraints on investment and production.
