ALTON urges states to scrap telecoms right-of-way fees

2026-10-28

The Association of Licensed Telecommunications Operators of Nigeria (ALTON) has called on state governments to abolish right-of-way charges for telecommunications infrastructure. The association says the fees can account for as much as 60 per cent of fibre-optic deployment costs in some states, making it harder to extend networks to underserved communities. Right-of-way charges are fees for permission to install infrastructure on public or private land and roads.

ALTON Chairman Gbenga Adebayo made the appeal on Tuesday at the 2026 Nigerian Economic Summit in Abuja. He said that in some states the charges represent 50 to 60 per cent of the cost of deploying a kilometre of fibre. That expense, he argued, can make projects difficult to justify in places where expected revenue is unlikely to cover construction costs.

Adebayo urged lawmakers to establish free access for telecommunications infrastructure, particularly in rural communities and other areas with limited commercial prospects. He also called for incentives for operators investing where deployment costs exceed likely returns. The Universal Service Provision Fund, which is intended to support areas lacking coverage, could be used more strategically, he said. Adebayo rejected the view that operators were unwilling to invest in rural areas, pointing instead to the economics of building networks there.

He also called for more investment in fibre-optic cables, telecommunications sites and network capacity, saying infrastructure is needed to improve service quality and support emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence. At the same summit, Senate ICT and Cybersecurity Committee Chairman Shuaib Salisu said the National Digital Economy and E-Government Bill had reached its third reading and that lawmakers were working on cybersecurity reforms and reviewing the Data Protection Act. He also pointed to the government’s planned 90,000-kilometre fibre network as a way to extend connectivity to underserved communities. Salisu urged businesses to engage lawmakers early so legislation reflects industry conditions and investment needs.
