NCDC identifies two suspected Ebola cases in North-East

2026-10-31

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention has identified two suspected Ebola cases in the North-East region. The individuals have been isolated, and health authorities are identifying and listing their contacts while laboratory tests are conducted to determine whether either has the disease.

The NCDC stressed that suspected-case classification does not mean the individuals have tested positive. Confirmation depends on laboratory results. The agency advised Nigerians to maintain good hand hygiene, improve sanitation and follow public health guidance, and said it would provide further updates as verified information becomes available.

The announcement comes after the Federal Government confirmed an imported Ebola case in Abuja on October 20, 2026. Health Minister Ali Pate said the woman had travelled from Uganda, developed symptoms on October 7 and sought care at a private hospital in Abuja on October 11. The source material does not state whether the two suspected cases in the North-East are connected to that case.

Authorities first identified 44 contacts of the woman, including healthcare workers, patients and caregivers; the number later rose to 53, who were placed under surveillance. The patient was evacuated to Switzerland on October 26 with her husband, who was asymptomatic. The World Health Organisation said a 42-day monitoring period would be required before Nigeria could be declared free of Ebola. The Federal Government also announced weekly payments of N100,000 to each contact to encourage compliance with quarantine directives.
