EFCC appeals judgement lifting forfeiture order on $912,550 linked to ex-Warri refinery MD

2026-11-12

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has appealed a Federal High Court ruling that rejected its bid to permanently forfeit $912,550 and other assets linked to Jimoh Olasunkanmi Yisawu, a former managing director of the Warri Refining and Petrochemical Company. Judge James Omotosho ruled on Monday that the commission had not proved the assets were proceeds of crime, lifted an interim forfeiture order on the funds and dismissed its application for final forfeiture. The EFCC said it filed a notice of appeal at the Court of Appeal in Abuja on Tuesday.

In its appeal, the commission argues that the judge failed to evaluate and properly weigh evidence about Yisawu’s income and financial transactions. It says the evidence showed that he gave $912,550 in cash to bureau de change operators Samaila Bala and Rasheed Yusuf between 2021 and 2025 for conversion to naira, with an equivalent value of more than N1.5 billion. The EFCC contends that Yisawu did not explain how he converted his lawful earnings into the foreign currency.

The commission also points to Yisawu’s declared cash and bank balances of N647,205.36 and $12,779.26 as of 17 September 2020, and says his salaries and allowances from October 2020 to September 2023 totalled N114,548,024.48. It further alleges that the dealers indirectly paid N282,542,000 towards developing two properties sought for forfeiture, and that Bala paid N65,860,000 to Cordros Securities Limited for treasury bills in three instalments in February 2024. The EFCC says these payments were not linked to Yisawu’s lawful earnings.

The EFCC is asking the appeal court to overturn the ruling and order final forfeiture of the funds and properties to the federal government. Yisawu, who worked for the Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation for 32 years before leaving in September 2023, told the lower court that he acquired the properties between 2015 and 2020 using earnings and loans. The forfeiture proceedings are separate from a criminal case in which he pleaded not guilty to eight money-laundering charges involving N1.339 billion. The EFCC’s allegations in both matters remain allegations; the appeal concerns the forfeiture ruling.
