Fake drinks may be causing liver, kidney diseases in Ondo — NMA

2026-10-28

The Ondo State chapter of the Nigerian Medical Association (NMA) has warned that suspected fake or adulterated beverages may be contributing to liver and kidney disease among young people. The association says doctors are encountering these conditions in age groups where they are considered uncommon, but has no confirmed count of cases linked to the drinks and says investigations are continuing.

Speaking in Akure, NMA state chairman Dr Roland Bola Arohunmolase said reported cases of liver cirrhosis among teenagers, and liver and kidney disease among people aged 22 to 30, warrant closer medical scrutiny. He identified unhealthy or adulterated beverages as a possible risk factor, while stressing that the causes of individual cases have not been established.

The association recalled earlier incidents involving suspected adulterated drinks in Odigbo and Irele, which reportedly caused at least 50 deaths, and an incident in Ilere linked to methanol consumption. It warned that methanol, an industrial chemical, can cause severe poisoning and organ damage if ingested. The NMA also said suspected counterfeit beverages may be found not only in open markets but in supermarkets, hotels, bars and other retail outlets.

The association urged the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission, the Ondo State Government and other relevant agencies to carry out unannounced inspections of outlets suspected of selling counterfeit drinks. It called for unsafe products to be seized and destroyed, and for those responsible for their production or distribution to be prosecuted. It also asked manufacturers to strengthen product authentication through measures such as tamper-proof seals, holograms, unique QR codes and tracking systems.

Consumers were advised to be cautious of drinks priced well below prevailing market rates and to check seals, labels and packaging before buying. Arohunmolase urged anyone who suspects they have consumed a fake or contaminated beverage to seek medical advice promptly rather than wait for symptoms to appear.
