Morning recap: Kane wins Ballon d’Or, EFCC arrests 15, other top stories

2026-10-27

Harry Kane has won the 2026 men’s Ballon d’Or, becoming the first Englishman to receive the award since Michael Owen in 2001. The Bayern Munich striker and England captain was recognised at a ceremony at the London Palladium after what was described as a prolific season for the German club.

In Ogun State, the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission arrested 14 Chinese nationals and one Nigerian at four alleged illegal lithium processing plants in Ogere. The commission said the operation followed intelligence gathering and surveillance. Investigators recovered lithium rocks, 18 mobile devices, four laptops and a trailer truck. The suspects are expected to face charges after the investigation is complete.

The Federal Government and organised labour are due to resume talks on workers’ demands, including lower petrol prices and a review of the national minimum wage. The Nigeria Labour Congress and Trade Union Congress have warned that a lack of progress could lead to nationwide industrial action. Separately, a prosecution witness told a Lagos court that social media personality Blessing Okoro, known as Blessing CEO, falsely claimed ownership of a property before collecting N69.15m. The witness cited WhatsApp conversations and other documents; Okoro has pleaded not guilty, and the case remains before the court.

The Senate has pledged to pass legislation establishing state police before the end of 2026, following approval of the constitutional amendment by the required number of state assemblies. The proposed system still has legislative steps to complete. Nigeria’s statutory revenue for the first 10 months of 2026 was N20.74tn below target, despite a sharp rise in crude oil prices. Music executive Don Jazzy has also announced a 2027 scholarship programme at TS Academy, offering eligible Nigerians up to full tuition coverage for technology and digital-skills courses. Laptop and data support may be available to successful applicants, but an application fee applies.
