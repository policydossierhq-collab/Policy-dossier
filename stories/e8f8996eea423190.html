Gov Abiodun declares Ogun ready for state police

2026-11-05

Ogun State Governor Dapo Abiodun said his administration was ready to establish a state police force, arguing that the state’s expanding industrial and commercial activity required stronger security. He made the announcement at a town hall meeting in Sagamu on the proposed 2027 budget and the 2027–2029 Medium Term Expenditure Framework. Abiodun chairs the Nigeria Governors’ Forum committee on state police. He said the state had supported its existing security network with vehicles, equipment and allowances, and increased recruitment into Amotekun. Amotekun and the So-Safe Corps together have about 6,000 personnel, he said.

The governor also announced the immediate start of a cash transfer programme for vulnerable households facing higher food and transport costs. He said recipients already identified in the state database in Sagamu would begin receiving payment alerts, with the programme later extending across Remoland and the wider state. Abiodun attributed some of the economic pressure to disruptions in global oil and gas supply chains linked to the Israel-American-Iran conflict, which he said had contributed to higher oil and transport costs.

Abiodun also outlined plans to sink boreholes in all 236 wards and provide transformers and solar-powered streetlights. He said Remoland was central to the state’s development plans. At the meeting, Economic Adviser and Finance Commissioner Dapo Okubadejo said public participation and accountability were important to governance. He reported expenditure of N490 billion against a N703 billion estimate in 2024, or about 70 per cent, and about N700 billion against the N1.05 trillion 2025 budget, or 65 per cent. The N1.67 trillion 2026 budget, he said, was supporting work in infrastructure, education, youth empowerment and agriculture.

Local representatives raised infrastructure and development concerns. Sagamu Local Government Chairman Afolabi Odulate called for action on roads in Sagamu, Remo North and Ikenne, and raised concerns about Lagos encroaching into Ogun through border communities. The Akarigbo of Remoland, Oba Babatunde Ajayi, welcomed development projects but urged the creation of a team to monitor and maintain public facilities. He also called for federal tertiary institutions in Remoland and stronger inclusion of local residents in economic initiatives. Remo’s Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria chair, Adewale Kalejaye, urged greater collaboration on revenue generation and more funding for poverty, hunger and education programmes.
